# Instagram Reels Algorithm

The **Instagram Reels algorithm** is a distribution test, not a personality. Instagram shows a new Reel to a small group, measures whether those people stay, and either widens the test or stops. This page covers that loop. For what to film, use [Instagram Reels content ideas](/blog/instagram-reels-content-ideas). For tags, use [Instagram Reels hashtags](/blog/instagram-reels-hashtags).

---

## The test, not the myth

A useful mental model:

1. **Seed.** A slice of people who already engage with you, plus some people in nearby topics.
2. **Hold.** Do they watch past the first second? Do they replay? Do they send it?
3. **Expand or die.** If the seed audience treats it as skippable, Instagram has no reason to spend more inventory on it.

Myths that waste time:

- A banned 'shadow' that explains every flop
- A guaranteed hashtag that 'beats' the algorithm
- Buying a trend sound as a substitute for a topic

If a Reel dies, the usual cause is a slow opening or a topic mismatch, not a secret penalty.

---

## Signals that actually move Reels

Instagram has described ranking as predicting how likely someone is to watch, like, comment, share, or follow. For Reels specifically, **watch time and sends** are the practical levers you can influence in the edit:

- **First-frame promise.** Text or a visual that states the payoff immediately.
- **Pattern interrupts.** A cut, zoom, or overlay change before attention drops.
- **Payoff that matches the promise.** If the caption says '3 mistakes' and you bury them at 0:40, people leave.
- **Sends.** Content people forward to one other person ('you need this') outperforms content people only double-tap.

Likes without watch time are a weak story. A Reel that is saved and sent with average likes is a stronger one.

---

## Original vs borrowed packaging

The algorithm is trying to match Reels to interests. That is easier when:

- You speak the topic in the first sentence
- Overlay text uses the same words
- The thumbnail-like first frame would make sense in search

Borrowed packaging (someone else's hook, someone else's audio, unrelated tags) makes matching harder. You can still use a popular audio track when it supports the scene. You should not use it as the entire strategy.

---

## Accounts, topics, and cold starts

A new account does not get a special forever boost. It gets tests. Those tests fail faster if every Reel is a different niche. Pick one topic family for a stretch of posts so Instagram has something to match.

Cold-start checklist:

- Profile name and bio name the same topic as the Reels
- Grid is not five unrelated businesses
- Each Reel can be described in one sentence a stranger would understand

## How to read a flop

Open Insights on the Reel (or the account's Reels tab):

- **Watched vs skipped in the first seconds.** If this is bad, rewrite the hook. Do not change hashtags first.
- **Replays.** A spike here often means a loopable payoff.
- **Follows from Reel.** Useful, but lagging. A Reel can distribute well without converting follows if the topic is entertainment-only.

Compare against your own median, not against a celebrity account. [ContHunt](https://conthunt.app) helps you inspect *public* Reels in the same niche so you can see how winners open. It does not expose Instagram's ranker, and it is not a claim that ContHunt runs the Reels algorithm.

## What to change next

| Symptom | Change first | Do not do first |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Drop in second 1-2 | Hook and first overlay | Hashtag swap |
| Watch holds, no sends | Sharper payoff / 'send this to X' utility | New audio |
| Wrong audience comments | Topic words + hashtags | Posting time |
| Good on followers, dead in Explore | Stronger topic clarity in speech | Buying a sound |

Then film the next Reel. The algorithm learns from a sequence, not from one resurrection attempt on an old post.
