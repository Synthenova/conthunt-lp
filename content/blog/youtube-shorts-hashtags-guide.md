# Best Hashtags for YouTube Shorts

Hashtags can connect a Short to related videos and hashtag search pages, but they are only one part of discovery. A relevant hashtag cannot rescue a vague title, misleading cover, or weak opening.

This guide separates YouTube's published rules from practical testing advice, then gives you starter sets you can adapt to the actual topic of your video.

---

## 1. What YouTube Officially Says About Hashtags

[YouTube's hashtag guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658?hl=en-on) says hashtags connect videos and playlists that share a topic and let viewers find related content through search. Creators can add them to a title or description, and YouTube may display up to three description hashtags above the title. YouTube does not publish an ideal count or promise that a particular hashtag will improve recommendations. It does warn that hashtags become less relevant as more are added; if a video or playlist has more than 60, every hashtag is ignored. Unrelated or misleading hashtags can also trigger removal under YouTube's metadata policies. The safe conclusion is simple: use only tags that accurately describe the video, keep the list short enough to stay focused, and treat hashtags as supporting metadata rather than a shortcut to reach.

A practical 3-to-5-tag starting point is an editorial test, not an official YouTube rule.

---

## 2. Research Hashtags From Relevant Videos

Static lists age quickly. Start with videos that are currently outperforming in the same niche, then record the topic language and recurring hashtags they use.

- Use **ContHunt** to find outlier short-form videos in your niche.
- Compare the tags on several winners instead of copying one viral post.
- Keep only tags that match your own video's subject and audience.
- Recheck the pattern as the topic changes.

This makes competitor research useful without pretending that a repeated tag caused the views.

## 4. Pair a Broad Topic With a Specific One

A broad tag can describe the category while a specific tag describes the actual subject. For example, pair `#Gaming` with `#MinecraftShorts` only when the Short is genuinely about Minecraft. The value is clarity for viewers and topic matching, not a published confidence score or guaranteed recommendation boost.

## 5. Hashtags Are Not Packaging

Hashtags help classify a Short, but they do not replace a strong title, thumbnail, or opening frame.

Hashtags classify a Short. They are not the whole packaging system. For hooks, captions, and retention, use the [short-form video best practices](/blog/short-form-video-best-practices) guide. For where to put tags, read [hashtags in the title vs the description](/blog/youtube-shorts-hashtags-title-vs-description) and [YouTube tags vs hashtags](/blog/youtube-tags-vs-hashtags).

Use this order:

1. Write the hook first.
2. Pick the title that states the promise clearly.
3. Choose or upload a readable cover.
4. Add a few accurate hashtags that confirm the topic.

For the packaging pieces, read [YouTube Title Character Limit 2026](/blog/youtube-title-character-limit-2026) and [YouTube Shorts Thumbnail Best Practices 2026](/blog/youtube-shorts-thumbnail-best-practices-2026).

## Conclusion
Hashtags in 2026 are useful for **clarity and related-topic discovery**, not guaranteed reach. Use a focused set that matches the video, follow YouTube's published rules, and spend more effort on the title, cover, hook, and viewer payoff.
