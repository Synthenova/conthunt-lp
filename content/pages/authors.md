ContHunt uses a small editorial team with topic-specific coverage. The goal is consistency between the person in the byline, the author schema, and the page body. Founder-led product context comes from Synthenova Enterprises LLP; the public library is written so a reader can see who is responsible for a guide.

## Who is on the team?

- [Zach Sanders](/zach-sanders), Head of Growth: Shorts distribution, packaging, and publishing cadence
- [Alex](/alex), Content Creator: hooks, formats, and creator-friendly examples
- [Elena](/elena), Head of Marketing: positioning, policy-aware pages, and narrative structure
- [Lamrin](/lamrin), Lead Engineer: product workflow, systems, and how analysis is built
- [Maya](/maya), Social Media Analyst: trends, performance patterns, and cross-platform notes

## How do we assign work?

We match authors to topics they already cover so the same person is not writing a legal page one day and a gaming-ideas page the next without a reason. Assignments are stored so rebuilds do not shuffle bylines. If an external subject-matter author is used, that person is named and linked.

## How should you read a byline?

A byline is not a celebrity endorsement. It tells you which specialist framed the research question. The page should still stand on sources, examples, and a workflow you can run. If the byline and the body disagree, email [support@conthunt.app](mailto:support@conthunt.app).

## What do authors not do?

Authors do not invent platform benchmarks, claim private access to another creator’s analytics, or promise that a hook will go viral. They document a method: collect public examples, keep the source, compare structure, and test one variable.
