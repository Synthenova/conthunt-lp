The best free TubeBuddy alternative depends on which paid habit you are trying not to fund. For general YouTube SEO, use YouTube Studio, especially the Research tab, and add vidIQ's free extension if you still want on-page tag help. For YouTube Shorts, use ContHunt to study public videos instead of paying an extension for tags. TubeBuddy's free tier, as this comparison describes it, is mostly basic tag suggestions and a best-time-to-publish calculation. Native thumbnail tests and Studio analytics now cover jobs that used to justify a paid extension for a small channel. Confirm those controls on your channel, because access can still vary. None of these free options bulk-edits a huge back catalog.

## What does TubeBuddy's free tier still cover?

TubeBuddy is a long-running YouTube extension for metadata, audits, and bulk processing. The free tier is the narrow slice of that product. This comparison treats the free tier as basic tag suggestions plus a best-time-to-publish hint. It does not treat the free tier as keyword research, library-wide description edits, or a Shorts strategy tool.

That boundary is the reason a "free TubeBuddy alternative" is usually a stack, not one logo. Tag hints answer a small on-page question. They do not tell you which subjects YouTube viewers search for, they do not test a thumbnail, and they do not explain why a Short kept or lost people. If your current use of TubeBuddy is the free extension and nothing else, you can replace that habit without a subscription. If your current use is bulk work across a large library, skip ahead to the limit section. Free tools do not become a bulk processor because a blog post wishes they would.

The paid product's reputation comes from housekeeping at scale: descriptions, links, end screens, and a health check for missing pieces. A zero-budget stack is honest only when it names the housekeeping it will not do.

## How far can YouTube Studio replace TubeBuddy?

YouTube Studio is the native free alternative, and it is the one teams skip because it is not an extension badge. Two Studio jobs matter for this decision.

The Research tab, inside analytics, is the search-demand view this guide trusts more than a third-party keyword score. Use it for keyword searches across YouTube and for content gaps, meaning subjects viewers look up that your channel has not covered in a way Studio can show you. Search data that comes from YouTube's own product is the right place to start a title or a topic list. An extension score is a second opinion, not the source of the searches.

Studio's analytics also cover the attention questions an extension sidebar often summarizes second-hand. Retention curves and audience overlap are the examples this guide cares about. Retention tells you where your own viewers leave. Audience overlap tells you which other channels share an audience with you. Neither number is available for a competitor's private analytics. Both numbers are available for you, without TubeBuddy, when your Studio access includes them.

Thumbnail tests are the other native replacement. This comparison treats YouTube's own thumbnail A/B testing as broadly available, including for creators who used to pay TubeBuddy mainly for that test. YouTube offering the test does not mean every channel sees the button today. Open Studio and check. If the test is there, stop paying an extension to reproduce it. If the test is not there, do not invent a workaround that pretends to be a controlled test. Change one variable at a time on later uploads and read retention, which is weaker evidence than a real test and should be labeled that way.

Studio will not bulk-rewrite a back catalog, suggest a tag while you type, or watch other people's Shorts for you. Those are the gaps the rest of the stack is for. Two of them have free or native answers. The bulk rewrite does not.

## What does vidIQ's free extension replace?

vidIQ's free extension is the browser-level TubeBuddy alternative this comparison prefers for on-page metadata. The free tier described here includes daily AI content ideas and competitor tracking for 3 rivals. That is a baseline, not an agency watchlist. Three rivals are enough to see whether your recent videos are in the same conversation as a few channels you chose. They are not a category census.

Daily ideas are useful when they name a topic you can film, and useless when they restate a trend you already rejected. Treat them as prompts. Check the prompt against Studio's Research tab before you trust it. If Studio does not show demand and you have no other reason to make the video, an idea from an extension is not demand.

Use the free extension for the moment you are writing a title, a description, or tags and you want a second list in the browser. Do not use it as proof that a Short will hold attention. Tags and descriptions are a weak lever on Shorts distribution compared with the opening and the watch. The extension can still save a few minutes of typing. Saving typing is not the same job as choosing the hook.

You can run TubeBuddy's free tier and vidIQ's free tier long enough to see which sidebar you actually click. This guide's default is vidIQ for that on-page habit, plus Studio for the numbers, because the TubeBuddy free tier as described here is the thinner of the two. Uninstall the one you do not click. Two extensions that rewrite the same YouTube page are not a strategy.

## How should Shorts research work without a paid extension?

YouTube Shorts growth is a retention and pattern problem more than a tag problem. A free TubeBuddy alternative for that problem has to show public videos, not another keyword grid. ContHunt searches TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Deep Search watches 500 to 1,000 videos in under five minutes and returns a ranked answer with the videos as evidence. That is the free-stack slot for "which openings are showing up, and what do the stronger public examples share?" It is not a Chrome extension that edits your title field, and this article does not quote a ContHunt price or plan name.

Use that research before you film, not as a replacement for Studio after you publish. The sequence is: pick a question, read public examples, choose one pattern, publish, then judge your own retention in Studio. [How Deep Search works](/resources/how-deep-search-works) is the step-by-step on the research half. [YouTube Shorts analytics](/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics) is the step-by-step on the owner-metrics half. Skipping either half leaves you with ideas you never test or with charts you cannot connect to a pattern.

A public research pass will not show your competitor's private retention curve. Say that in the brief so nobody treats a view count on someone else's Short as your target completion. Your completion, rewatches, and traffic sources live in your analytics. Their video is evidence of what was made, not of how long strangers stayed.

## Where does Google Trends fit?

Google Trends is the free look at search interest outside YouTube. Use it when you suspect a subject is moving in web search before it is crowded on YouTube, or when you want to know whether a topic is a YouTube habit only. It will not score a YouTube title, suggest tags, or test a thumbnail. It is a macro check, not an SEO extension.

A practical use: you have a Studio Research suggestion and a vidIQ daily idea that disagree. Look the subject up in Google Trends for direction, not for a score. Rising interest outside YouTube is a reason to consider the topic. A flat line is a reason to ask whether YouTube demand in Studio is enough on its own. Neither result is a promise of views. Trends does not know your retention.

Do not stack Trends, Studio, vidIQ, and a research tool into four opinions of equal weight. Studio answers YouTube search. Trends answers broader search interest. vidIQ's free tier answers on-page help and a three-rival snapshot. ContHunt answers public short-form patterns. If two of them conflict, believe the one whose question you actually asked.

## What does a zero-budget stack contain?

A zero-budget YouTube stack in this guide is four tools with four jobs. Cost here means money. Time is not free, which is why the weekly list is short.

| Tool | Job it covers | Money | What it will not do |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube Studio Research | YouTube search terms and content gaps | No subscription | Bulk-edit old descriptions |
| YouTube Studio analytics | Your retention, audience overlap, and results | No subscription | A competitor's private curve |
| YouTube thumbnail test, if shown | A controlled thumbnail comparison | No subscription | A test the button does not offer |
| vidIQ free extension | On-page ideas and 3 tracked rivals | Free tier as described | A full category watchlist |
| Google Trends | Interest outside YouTube | No subscription | Title scores or tags |
| ContHunt | Public TikTok, Reels, and Shorts research | Not a TubeBuddy price comparison | Editing your YouTube metadata |

The table includes six rows because two of them are Studio features, not extra vendors. The point of a free TubeBuddy alternative is fewer subscriptions, not fewer steps. If you only adopt one change, make it the Research tab plus your own retention graph. Those two screens retire more paid habits than a new extension does.

## What should you do each week?

Run this once a week. It replaces the feeling that you should "check the extension."

1. Open Studio Research and write down up to five searches or gaps that match the channel's subject. Discard the rest. A long list is not a calendar.
2. For one search you might film, draft a title in your own words. Only then look at the free extension's suggestion. Keep yours if the suggestion is vaguer.
3. If you are choosing a thumbnail and Studio offers a test, start the test. If it does not, note that you are changing one variable without a formal test.
4. For Shorts, write the opening line before you look at tags. If you need examples, search public videos for that subject rather than adding tag variants.
5. After publishing, read retention on the new video against your recent ones. A free stack fails when you never return to Studio.
6. Once a month, ask whether any paid TubeBuddy feature would have changed a decision you actually made. If the answer is bulk edits you keep postponing, that is a paid-tool conversation. If the answer is "not really," stay on the free stack.

Illustrative time box, not a measured average: Research tab for 20 minutes, one title decision for 15 minutes, one public-pattern check for 25 minutes, and a retention read for 15 minutes. That is 20 + 15 + 25 + 15 = 75 minutes. If your week cannot spare that, cut the extension step first. Studio and the opening line are the parts that change the video.

## How do you test whether the free stack is enough?

Do not switch tools and declare victory. Run a four-video check. The counts below are a design, not results.

Pick four upcoming videos in one subject. For two of them, choose the topic and title from Studio Research only. For the other two, start from a vidIQ free-tier idea, then accept it only if Research also supports it. Publish all four through your normal process. A week after each one, record the same Studio fields, such as views and the retention shape, in one note. You are looking for whether the Research-only pair is obviously weaker. If it is not, the extension is optional. If the extension-led pair is stronger and Research agrees with those topics, keep the extension as a prompt list and keep Research as the filter.

Do not compute a "lift" from four videos and call it a channel law. Four videos can be noise. The test only needs to answer "am I paying attention to a sidebar I do not need?" Illustrative record-keeping, so the sheet stays honest:

| Video | Topic source | Title source | Retention note after a week |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Studio Research | Your draft | Write what you see, no target |
| 2 | Studio Research | Your draft | Same fields as video 1 |
| 3 | vidIQ idea, Research agreed | Extension draft you edited | Same fields |
| 4 | vidIQ idea, Research agreed | Your draft anyway | Same fields |

If video 4 holds people as well as video 3, you do not need the extension's title. That is the result that lets you stay free without guessing.

## When is a paid TubeBuddy plan still the rational buy?

Pay when the library is the problem. Bulk description, link, and end-screen updates across a large catalog are the jobs this comparison still assigns to TubeBuddy rather than to a free stack. A health check for broken links or missing metadata is the same family of work. YouTube Studio will not do that pass for you as a bulk edit. vidIQ's free tier will not either.

Do not pay for a keyword explorer, a thumbnail test, or a best-time widget if Studio already answers those questions for your channel. Do not pay for Shorts growth if the missing piece is hook research. Read [TubeBuddy review](/blog/tubebuddy-review) before you upgrade from the free tier, and use the free stack in this article as the thing you compare the upgrade against. A paid plan is relevant when it removes hours of library work you would otherwise do by hand. It is not relevant because an extension has been on the channel for years.

The free path is Studio for search and retention, a native thumbnail test when you have it, vidIQ's free extension for on-page help and three rivals, Google Trends when the question is outside YouTube, and ContHunt when the question is public short-form patterns. Keep TubeBuddy's paid tools for bulk housekeeping if that job is real. Otherwise stop paying for features the free stack already covers, and [Get started]({{GET_STARTED_URL}}) on the public-video question your extension cannot see.
