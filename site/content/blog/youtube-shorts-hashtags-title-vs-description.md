Put YouTube Shorts hashtags in the description first, and keep the title as a sentence a person can read. Written by the ContHunt team. YouTube lets creators add hashtags to the title or the description, and both link to a results page. Out of the description hashtags, up to three that YouTube considers most engaging can appear by the title. Extra tags can still sit in the description. They are not all shown. YouTube does not say a title hashtag ranks higher. A stuffed title still costs the click, because the promise is harder to read. If a video or playlist has more than 60 hashtags, YouTube ignores every hashtag on that content. This page decides the slot, not which tags to use.

## What does YouTube document about hashtag placement?

YouTube documents a small placement rule for hashtags, and that rule is the whole official answer. [Find playlists and videos using hashtags](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658) says you can add hashtags to a title and a description. You can do that when you upload a video or record a Short. Hashtags in the title and in the description link to a results page. Out of all the hashtags in the description, up to three that are considered most engaging appear by the video title. The hashtags still appear in the description, and the videos can still appear in search results. YouTube does not publish a sentence that says "title hashtags rank higher."

The same page is strict about volume and honesty. Hashtags do not contain spaces. If you want two words, join them, as in `#TwoWords`. If a video or playlist has more than 60 hashtags, YouTube ignores each hashtag on that content. Over-tagging may also result in removal from uploads or from search. Misleading or unrelated hashtags can be removed under YouTube's misleading metadata policies. Adding ordinary descriptive tags or repetitive sentences in the description is still prohibited, even though hashtags are allowed. Harassment, hate, sexual content, and vulgar language in hashtags have their own removal or age-restriction consequences on that page. Placement cannot rescue a tag that should not be on the video.

Everything else on this page is testing practice. A practical block of a few accurate tags is an editorial habit. It is not an official count.

## Why is the description the default slot?

The description is the default slot for YouTube Shorts hashtags because the title should stay a readable promise. Use the description-only pattern unless you have a specific reason not to. An example of the fields, not a script you must copy.

```
Title: I stopped using 12 Shorts hashtags

Description:
What I use instead of a 12-tag block, and why the first three matter.

#shorts #youtubeshorts #contentstrategy
```

That default wins for three practical reasons. The title stays a sentence a human can read in search and on the Shorts shelf. You still get clickable hashtag pages from the description, and YouTube may lift up to three of those description hashtags by the title. You are less likely to trip an unrelated-metadata problem with a title that is mostly tags. The number 12 in the example title is part of the sentence. It is not a recommended tag count, and it is not a count of tags you should paste anywhere.

Write the description as a sentence first and the tags after it. The sentence tells a viewer, and YouTube's own metadata rules, what the Short is about. The tags confirm the topic. A description that is only a hashtag block throws away the sentence. YouTube may still show up to three tags by the title, but you have given search and the shelf nothing to read.

## When is one hashtag in the title reasonable?

One hashtag in the YouTube Shorts title is reasonable only when the rest of the title still reads as ordinary language. A single format tag can stay if the promise remains intact. This title does that:

```
How I cut a 12-hour vlog into a Short #Shorts
```

The clause before the tag says what the video does. The tag does not replace the clause. Drop the title tag when the title is already long. Drop it when you would need two or more hashtags in the title. Also drop it when the hashtag repeats a word already in the title, as in `Shorts ideas #shorts`. Repetition spends characters you needed for the payoff. Titles can be crowded on a phone long before you hit any character ceiling. Spend the visible words on the promise.

Never lead with a tag dump. This is not a title:

```
#shorts #viral #fyp How I edit
```

That line is a tag dump with a leftover clause. It hides the only words a viewer could use to decide. `#viral` and `#fyp` are also a test of YouTube's "directly related" rule. If the Short is not about those topics, the tags are the kind of unrelated metadata the help page says may be removed. Do not put them in the title to borrow an audience.

## How do title and description jobs differ?

Title and description jobs differ because they fail in different places. The title carries the promise, with at most one optional format tag. The first lines of the description carry context. A short hashtag block in the description carries a few accurate tags. Spoken words and on-screen text should use the same topic words as that package. If the title says one subject and the tags say another, you are asking YouTube to guess. Guessing usually means weaker matching, not a secret extra audience.

| Placement | Use it for | What goes wrong |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Title | The promise, plus an optional single format tag | Unreadable text on the shelf or in search |
| Description, first lines | A sentence of context that matches the video | Nothing, if the sentence is true |
| Description, hashtag block | A few accurate tags, not a dump | Noise, or every tag ignored past 60 |
| Spoken line and on-screen text | The same topic words as the title | A mismatch the viewer can see immediately. |

An illustrative mismatch, not a case study: the title says the Short is an Excel shortcut, and the hashtag block says Minecraft. Those are two videos. Publishing them as one metadata set does not reach spreadsheet viewers and game viewers. It reaches neither cleanly. Pick the subject you filmed. Describe that subject in the title, the first description sentence, the on-screen words, and the tags.

YouTube's separate tags field is not this hashtag decision. Tags and hashtags are different metadata. How they differ is covered in [YouTube tags vs hashtags](/blog/youtube-tags-vs-hashtags). Which words belong in the hashtag block is covered in [best hashtags for YouTube Shorts](/blog/youtube-shorts-hashtags-guide). This page only chooses the field.

## How do you test title hashtags without fooling yourself?

You test a title hashtag with a paired upload. You cannot A/B one live Short the way you can A/B a long-form thumbnail. Run the pair on two similar Shorts, not on two unrelated topics.

1. Use the same hook family and the same length band on both Shorts.
2. On Short A, put the hashtags in the description only.
3. On Short B, put one format hashtag in the title and keep the same description tags.
4. Compare click-through on the title, where YouTube shows it, and compare average view duration.
5. If Short B's title is truncated or looks like spam on a phone, stop using title hashtags even when the view counts are similar.

The click cost is real even when the tag is allowed. Similar view counts do not excuse a title nobody can read.

Here is illustrative arithmetic so the comparison has a shape. It is not a result from a channel. Suppose the title click-through YouTube shows is 16 clicks out of 80 times Short A's title was presented. The rate is 16 ÷ 80 = 0.20, or 20%. Suppose Short B shows 10 clicks out of 80. The rate is 10 ÷ 80 = 0.125, or 12.5%. The gap is 20 − 12.5 = 7.5 percentage points. If your real pair is that far apart, and the only deliberate change was the title tag, prefer the description-only title. Use it on the next upload in that hook family. If the rates are close and the title is harder to read, prefer the readable title anyway. The test is there to stop you stuffing both fields out of habit. It is not there to crown a tag.

Do not "win" the test by changing the hook, the length, and the topic at the same time. That is three tests. You will not know which one moved the click-through.

## What order should Shorts metadata follow?

Shorts metadata should follow a fixed order so the hashtag slot is the last decision, not the first. Do the hook and the first frame. Write a readable title. Write the description sentence. Add a small hashtag set in the description. Add an optional title hashtag only if the title still reads as English. That order matches the hashtag guide. This page does not pick the words. It stops the words from landing in the wrong field.

A checklist before you publish:

- The title is a sentence, and it states the promise without a tag dump.
- There is at most one hashtag in the title, and the title still makes sense if you delete it.
- The description opens with a sentence that matches the footage.
- The description hashtags accurately describe this Short, not a neighboring trend.
- You are nowhere near 60 hashtags. Passing 60 means YouTube ignores every hashtag, not merely the extras.
- The title and the tags name the same subject.
- You looked at the title on a phone before you decided the title tag was fine.

Delete the title tags, keep one readable sentence, and put a few accurate hashtags under the description sentence.
