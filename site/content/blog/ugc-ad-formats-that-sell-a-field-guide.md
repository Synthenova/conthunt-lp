UGC ad formats that sell are short videos commissioned to look like ordinary posts, picked for what the viewer already knows and built from beats a creator can film. TikTok's own [creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices) recommend featuring creators, employees or customers and going for a DIY look that is "not overly polished", so the format fits the feed it runs in.

After the offer, the format is the biggest lever a UGC buyer controls. Pick the wrong one and a good product reads as a sales pitch. The seven formats below are the set to choose from, along with a way to decide between them and what to settle with a creator before they hit record.

## Choose the format by what the viewer already knows

Choosing a UGC format starts from what the viewer already knows, because a person who only feels the problem and a person who already knows your product need different proof. Copywriter Eugene Schwartz described levels of buyer awareness in *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966). A short version works for UGC planning:

| Viewer knows... | They need | Start with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The problem, not the solution | To see the problem solved | Problem-solution demo, routine integration |
| That solutions exist, not yours | A reason to pick yours | Comparison, expert explainer |
| Your product, not why to buy now | Proof and risk removal | Honest review, unboxing, comment reply |

Run one format per audience stage in your first test. Mixing formats and audiences in one ad set makes the result unreadable.

## The seven formats

The seven formats are problem-solution demo, honest review, unboxing, routine integration, comparison, comment reply and expert explainer, and each one has a use, a beat sheet, a brief line and a failure mode. Pick one before you write the script.

### 1. Problem-solution demo

A problem-solution demo is a 20 to 30 second UGC ad that films a visible problem, the product in use, and the result, for a viewer who already feels the pain.

- **Use when** the product fixes something visible and the buyer already feels the pain.
- **Beats** (20 to 30 seconds): problem on camera (0 to 3s), product enters and is used (3 to 15s), result shown (15 to 25s), one line of next step.
- **Brief line:** "Open on the problem happening for real. Show the product in use before you explain it. End on the result, close up."
- **Watch for** staged-looking problems. Messy is believable.

### 2. Honest review

An honest review is a 30 to 45 second UGC ad that gives the verdict in the first sentence, includes one real downside, and says who should not buy.

- **Use when** the buyer is product-aware and comparing, or the price needs justifying.
- **Beats** (30 to 45s): verdict first (0 to 3s), what you expected, what happened, one downside, who it is for.
- **Brief line:** "Give your verdict in the first sentence. Include one real downside. Say who should not buy it."
- **Watch for** reviews with no downside. Viewers read them as paid.

### 3. Unboxing and first impressions

An unboxing is a 20 to 40 second UGC ad that films the first open, one reaction, and three details of size, feel or smell, for products where the package is part of the appeal.

- **Use when** the packaging, scale or contents are part of the appeal: gifting, bundles, subscription kits.
- **Beats** (20 to 40s): package arrival, one reaction line, three details the creator notices, first use.
- **Brief line:** "Film the first time you open it. Say what surprises you about size, feel or smell."
- **Watch for** long openings. Start at the reveal, not at the courier.

### 4. Routine integration

A routine integration is a 25 to 40 second UGC ad that places the product as one step in a habit the creator already has, such as a morning routine.

- **Use when** the product lives inside a habit: skincare, supplements, cooking, pet care, cleaning.
- **Beats** (25 to 40s): situation ("my 7am routine"), product appears naturally, one detail on how it fits, outcome after the habit.
- **Brief line:** "Show the product as one step in a routine you already do. Mention it as you would a friend's recommendation."
- **Watch for** a routine so busy the product disappears. Keep it in frame for at least five seconds.

### 5. Comparison: "I tried three"

A comparison is a 30 to 45 second UGC ad that tests crowded-category options under the same conditions and ranks them without naming a competitor negatively.

- **Use when** the category is crowded and the buyer is solution-aware.
- **Beats** (30 to 45s): the test setup (0 to 5s), each option under the same conditions, a ranking, why yours won.
- **Brief line:** "Test all options the same way, on camera. Do not name a competitor negatively. Show the measurement."
- **Watch for** rigged tests. Run it for real, and keep the footage.

### 6. Comment reply

A comment reply is a UGC ad that answers one real buyer comment on camera, for a viewer who already knows the product and needs a specific objection removed.

- **Use when** you have an objection that keeps appearing in comments: "does it leave residue?", "is it loud?"
- **Beats** (15 to 25s): the comment on screen (0 to 2s), direct answer, demonstration, short line to close.
- **Brief line:** "Reply to this exact comment: [paste]. Answer in one sentence, then prove it."
- **Watch for** invented comments. Reply to real ones.

### 7. Expert explainer

An expert explainer is a UGC ad in which a person with relevant experience teaches one mechanism of the product, for a viewer who knows solutions exist but not why this one works.

- **Use when** the product claims a mechanism the buyer does not understand: ingredients, materials, technique.
- **Beats** (30 to 50s): credential and claim (0 to 5s), explanation with a simple visual, one limitation, recommendation.
- **Brief line:** "State your real experience in the first line. Explain the mechanism in one visual. Name one thing the product cannot do."
- **Watch for** credentials that go beyond what the creator holds, and for claims that need substantiation.

## Where to find the formats that work in your category

The formats that work in your category are the ones that dominate the strongest recent videos, counted against each creator's own median rather than copied from a mood board. Before briefing, run the same exercise as in the [hook research guide](/resources/research-tiktok-hooks-before-you-brief-a-creator), applied to format. Pull a recent set across TikTok, Reels and Shorts, normalize views against each creator's median, and count formats in the top quartile against the whole set. In ContHunt, a Deep Search prompt does this in one pass:

> For kitchen gadgets in the last 30 days, label each video's format (problem-solution demo, honest review, unboxing, routine integration, comparison, comment reply, expert explainer, other). Show the format mix of the top quartile versus all videos, with example links.

## What to settle before the creator records

Five points get settled before the creator records: usage rights, disclosure, claims, real people and the files you expect back. Leave any one of them implied and the footage is hard to run.

**Usage rights.** Write down where the footage may run: organic repost, paid ads, how long, which countries. For paid use on TikTok, [Spark Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/spark-ads) can promote another creator's post only with that creator's authorization, so ask for it in the agreement.

**Disclosure.** The FTC's [guidance for social media influencers](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers) says the disclosure belongs in the video itself, not only in the description, and works best in both audio and video. Plain terms such as "ad" or "sponsored" are acceptable. Vague tags like "collab" are not.

**Claims.** The creator may only say what you can substantiate. List forbidden claims in the brief, especially for health, skin and money products.

**Real people only.** The FTC's [final rule on fake reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) covers AI-generated fake reviews. Do not present synthetic "customers" as real ones.

**Deliverables.** Ask for 9:16 files, raw footage, and three alternative openings on one body.

## Test in this order

Tests run in a fixed order: hook first, then format, then offer, and each test changes one variable with a hypothesis written before launch. A test with no hypothesis teaches nothing when it loses.

1. **Hook.** Same body, three openings. This is the cheapest test and usually the most informative.
2. **Format.** Same offer and audience, different format from the list above.
3. **Offer and call to action.** Only after hook and format settle.

Change one variable per test, and write the hypothesis before launch.

## Brief template you can reuse for any format

The brief template records one of the seven formats, the audience stage, the job, the timed beats, three openings, the rights and the disclosure, so the creator is not guessing which video to shoot. Copy it and fill the brackets from the format you picked.

```text
FORMAT: [one of the seven]
AUDIENCE STAGE: [problem-aware / solution-aware / product-aware]
JOB: [Audience] should [believe/do] because they see [proof].
BEATS: [timed beats from the format above]
OPENING VARIANTS: A / B / C
MUST SHOW: [product use, proof moment]
MUST NOT SAY: [claims list]
RIGHTS: [platforms, paid use, duration, authorization]
DISCLOSURE: in video, audio and text
DELIVER: 9:16, raw footage, 3 openings
```

## Match the format to proof you can film

A format sells only when its beats contain proof you can film with this product and this creator. Write the one moment the viewer must see, then keep the format whose beats include that moment. A format that needs a result you cannot show, a downside you will not allow, or a credential the creator does not hold will look like an ad even when the shooting style stays loose.

Walk the seven formats against the footage you actually have:

- **Problem-solution demo** needs the problem happening for real and a result in the same clip. The beats run about 20 to 30 seconds, with the problem in the first 3 seconds and the result later in the clip. If the true result takes weeks, do not pretend it fits inside that window. Show the step you can film in the session, such as texture, fit or a side-by-side under the same light, and save the longer claim for a format that can carry it.
- **Honest review** needs a verdict in the first sentence and one real downside, inside a clip of about 30 to 45 seconds. If the brief forbids every downside, pick another format. The failure mode is already stated above: viewers read a review with no downside as paid.
- **Unboxing** needs a first opening. It fits when packaging, scale or contents are part of the appeal, in about 20 to 40 seconds, starting at the reveal rather than at the courier. It is a weak default when the buyer already understands the product.
- **Routine integration** needs a habit the creator already does. The beats run about 25 to 40 seconds, and the product has to stay in frame for at least five seconds. A routine so busy that the product disappears fails this format even if the habit is real.
- **Comparison** needs every option under the same conditions and a measurement the viewer can see, in about 30 to 45 seconds. The brief line already says not to name a competitor negatively. If you cannot run the test for real and keep the footage, do not brief "I tried three".
- **Comment reply** needs a real comment on screen in the first 2 seconds of a 15 to 25 second clip. Paste that comment into the brief. An invented comment fails the format and the trust the reply is supposed to earn.
- **Expert explainer** needs a credential the creator actually holds and one limitation they can say out loud. The beats run about 30 to 50 seconds, with the credential and claim in the first 5 seconds. The claims list in the brief still bounds the explanation, especially for health, skin and money products.

After the format is real, keep the audience stage from the table above and write three openings on that same body. The test order in this guide separates hook, format and offer. Changing the format and the opening in one shoot makes a loss impossible to read, because you will not know which change the viewer responded to. The template has one line for the format and one line for the three openings so those decisions stay apart. Rights, disclosure and the ban on fake testimonials still apply to whichever format you pick. They are not a reason to choose a format. They are the conditions under which any of the seven can run.
