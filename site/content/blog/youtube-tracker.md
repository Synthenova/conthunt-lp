A YouTube tracker organizes channel or video numbers so you can answer one question, and the right tool depends on which question you have. Written by the ContHunt team. YouTube Studio is for a channel you manage. Social Blade and vidIQ's YouTube Stats show public subscriber and view totals for channels you do not manage. Viewstats is for video and competitor research. ContHunt is not a live subscriber counter and not a source of another creator's private analytics. It searches public short-form video for creative patterns. This comparison uses public product pages and YouTube documentation reviewed for the guide on September 9, 2026. It is not a hands-on test of every paid feature, and it does not assign an overall score.

## What is a YouTube tracker?

A YouTube tracker stores or displays channel and video metrics, such as public subscribers and views. Some products also keep those observations over time. Tracker is a job description, not a single product category. One product answers what happened on your own channel. Another answers what public totals that channel shows. A third answers which videos are worth watching before you film. Mixing those jobs is how a public estimate gets quoted as Studio revenue.

Before you choose, name the data class:

- An authorized channel report is what YouTube Studio shows the people who manage the channel. It can include traffic sources, watch behavior, and, for eligible channels, revenue.
- A public observation is something a viewer can see or a public page records. Examples are a view total, a like count, a comment, or a publish date.
- A modeled estimate is a figure the tool calculates, such as a displayed earnings guess. It is not a payment receipt.

Check the source, the update rhythm, and how far back the history goes. A chart with no timestamp is not yet a tracker you can compare week to week. A public counter that refreshes daily is not a live subscriber feed, even if the page feels current. [YouTube Shorts analytics](/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics) is the workflow for reading your own Shorts reports once you are inside Studio. This page is about choosing which tool is allowed to answer which question.

## Which tool matches which question?

Choose the YouTube tracker that holds the evidence for the question, and stop when a tool would have to guess. The starting points below are public entry points, not a ranking.

| Your question | Where to start | What to verify before you trust a figure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How did my channel perform? | [YouTube Studio](https://studio.youtube.com/) | The report, the date range, and YouTube's definition of the metric |
| What public subscriber and view totals does a channel show? | [Social Blade](https://socialblade.com/youtube) | Whether that channel is covered, and the time of the observation |
| How do public channel statistics compare? | [vidIQ YouTube Stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/) | The update frequency. Treat any earnings figure as an estimate |
| Which videos, thumbnails, or competitor patterns should I study? | [Viewstats](https://www.viewstats.com/) | Which research features, alerts, or history require an account |
| Which public short-form patterns show up across platforms? | ContHunt | The original public videos, not a private retention file |

ContHunt publishes this guide and is one of the products in the table. That is a disclosure, not a claim that ContHunt replaces a channel tracker. The short-form research job is a different job from subscriber history. If your question is "how many subscribers did they gain yesterday," ContHunt is the wrong row. If your question is "what do the public Shorts in this niche do in the opening seconds," a subscriber tracker is the wrong row.

## What does YouTube Studio track on your own channel?

YouTube Studio tracks the analytics of a channel you manage, including the reports YouTube exposes for that channel's videos. For Shorts, YouTube's [content tab analytics guide](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?hl=en&co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts) is the documentation for those content reports. The guide frames a viewer's path as appeal, engagement, and satisfaction. Appeal is whether the video catches attention when it is recommended, which YouTube ties to impressions click-through rate. Engagement is whether people then watch, which YouTube ties to views. Satisfaction is whether they watch for a meaningful stretch and have a reason to return, which YouTube ties to average view duration.

On that content tab, an impression is how many times your video thumbnails were shown to viewers on YouTube, not on external sites or apps. Impressions click-through rate is how often viewers watched a video after seeing the thumbnail. YouTube says that rate indicates how appealing the thumbnail, the title, and the video are to the people who saw them. Views are how many times the content was viewed, set against the videos' typical performance. Average view duration is the average minutes watched per view for the filters you set. The same guide says to compare videos of the same type. Audience behavior differs across formats. The content tab can be filtered so Shorts, longer videos, live, and posts are not mashed into one list.

Those definitions are why Studio is the tracker for your own packaging tests. A cover change is an appeal question, and impressions and click-through rate are the Studio metrics attached to that question. A weak opening is an engagement and satisfaction question. None of those reports transfer to a competitor. Managing your channel does not unlock another channel's retention, revenue, or audience report. When you need a practical review habit for your own Shorts, stay inside Studio. Keep the reporting window the same from week to week. A change in the window is not a change in the video.

Studio is also where YouTube will show the search terms it reports for your channel, through traffic-source reports. The content guide describes YouTube search as a list of terms and words audiences used to find your content. That list is evidence about your channel. It is not a global keyword database, and it is not what a public subscriber tracker is for.

## What do public channel pages actually show?

Social Blade and vidIQ's YouTube Stats show public channel statistics, not the private Studio of the channel you looked up. [Social Blade's YouTube page](https://socialblade.com/youtube) displays channel rankings with subscribers, views, and video counts. It is a reasonable first stop when you need to find a channel and see the public scale of it. Coverage is not guaranteed for every channel, and a historical chart is only as useful as the observations that channel's page actually stores. Look at the specific channel and the timestamp. A public total is not a promise of exact subscriber changes second by second. An earnings figure on a public page is not the money the channel received.

[vidIQ's YouTube Stats](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/) offers channel search and subscriber rankings. The page describes subscribers, views, earnings, and growth as updated daily. Daily is the rhythm to quote. A daily refresh is not a live counter, and it should not be narrated as if subscribers were ticking in real time. Earnings displayed there are estimates. The public statistics page is also not the same thing as every other feature elsewhere in vidIQ, including anything that sits behind a paid plan. This guide does not list plan names or prices. Confirm the access you need on vidIQ's own site before you depend on an export, an alert, or a history view.

Use these pages to record public scale. Do not use them to claim you know a competitor's watch time. If the decision you actually need is about keywords or what to publish next, that is a research workflow, not a subscriber ranking. A ranking table and a keyword workflow answer different questions even when one company sells both.

## Where do Viewstats and ContHunt fit?

Viewstats and ContHunt both help you decide what to study, and neither one is your Studio login. [Viewstats](https://www.viewstats.com/) describes tools for finding outlier videos, researching channel and video performance, comparing thumbnails, following competitors, and creating alerts and research boards. That set of jobs matters when the question is what to make next, not merely how large a subscriber count is. Confirm on the vendor's current site which of those features your access includes. A vendor's statement that a method grows channels is not evidence that using the tool will make your next video travel. Outlier-finding is a way to choose examples. It is not a causal proof that the outlier's thumbnail, length, or title produced the outlier.

ContHunt is a content intelligence product for finding and analyzing public short-form video on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Use it to inspect choices a viewer can see: the opening, the pacing, the on-screen words, the topic, and the payoff. Deep Search watches 500 to 1,000 videos in under five minutes and returns a ranked answer with the videos as the evidence. That ranking is an analysis of the public videos against the question you asked. It is not another creator's private watch time, and it is not a subscriber ledger. ContHunt tracking is experimental. It is not a finished live YouTube subscriber tracker, and it should not be described as one.

A focused way to watch other Shorts without pretending you have their Studio is [how to track YouTube Shorts competitors](/blog/how-to-track-youtube-shorts-competitors). The limit stays the same there. Public videos, public counts, and creative patterns are in bounds. Retention curves and revenue are not.

## Why do two trackers disagree on views?

Two YouTube trackers can disagree on views because they are not always counting the same event, at the same time, under the same definition. [How engagement metrics are counted](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2991785?hl=en) says that beginning August 24, 2026, views are counted the moment a video starts to play. The rule covers Shorts, long-form videos, and live streams. When you compare a figure from before that date with a figure from after it, label the metric and the period. Do not assume every third-party tracker adopted the same definition on the same day, or that it refreshed when YouTube did.

The same page says the change does not affect YouTube Partner Program earnings or eligibility. Earnings stay based on engaged views and engaged watch hours. Eligibility stays based on qualified views. A public view total and a monetization total are different series. A tracker that displays views, and a Studio revenue tab that displays earnings, are not two readings of one number. Quoting the public view count as if it were qualified views mixes those series. Quoting an estimated earnings badge as if it were an AdSense payment does the same.

YouTube also says real-time analytics are estimates of potential view activity and might not match the number on the watch page. A temporary gap between Studio's realtime report and the public count is not, by itself, proof that either display is broken. Metrics can take time to appear during the first few hours after publishing, because YouTube is filtering for human interaction rather than automated playback. Verified metrics can take several days to update fully in Earn tab milestones. On some videos, counts are slowed, frozen, or adjusted while low-quality playbacks are removed. YouTube's examples of low-quality playback include watching the same video on several devices and streaming it across several windows and tabs.

Paid views are a further split on that page. Billable TrueView views follow ad rules. YouTube public views of ads can contribute to the public view count. Those views are counted when a video ad starts to play. The rule covers Shorts and in-stream ads. A tracker that mixes ad views and organic views without saying so is not comparable to a Studio report you filtered to one traffic source.

[Reading virality beyond views](/resources/reading-virality-beyond-views) is the companion for why a raw view total is a weak comparison even after the definition is stable. Use it when the argument in the room is that more views means a better video. That comparison fails when the videos differ in age, format, or account size.

## What can you not see on a competitor?

A public YouTube tracker cannot show a competitor's private watch time, retention, revenue, or audience breakdown. Those live in the Studio of the channel that owns them. Public views, likes, comments, and publish dates are observations. Earnings badges and audience models are estimates unless the channel owner has shared the underlying report. There is no setting in a public tool that turns an estimate into that report.

This limit changes how you keep notes. Write down the URL, the time you looked, and the exact label on the page. "Subscribers" on a public ranking is not "subscribers gained in the last seven days" unless the page says it is a change over a period. A total and a delta answer different questions. If the page does not say whether Shorts and long-form videos are split, do not invent the split. Compare a Short with a Short when you can see the format. YouTube's own content guide tells channel owners to do that because behavior differs by format. The same caution applies when the numbers came from outside Studio.

A creative observation is still available. You can watch the public video and describe the opening, the title, and the cover. You cannot watch the public video and know the percentage of viewers who left at a given second. Keep those sentences in different paragraphs so a later reader does not promote a hypothesis into a metric.

## How do you run a week of tracking without mixing metrics?

A weekly YouTube tracking habit records the same fields, from the same sources, and refuses to explain a change you did not time. Two snapshots show the difference between those two moments. They do not show the hour the change happened, and they do not show which creative choice caused it.

1. In Studio, record the same metrics for your own channel on the same weekday, with the same date range and the same format filter.
2. Pick a small set of public channels that are actually in your topic. A huge list becomes a scrapbook.
3. For each public figure, store the source URL, the observation time, and the label as the page prints it.
4. Separate videos by format, age, and topic before you compare them. A Short from yesterday and a long video from last year are not a pair.
5. Watch a few public videos that stand out and write down visible choices only.
6. Change one variable in your next video. Read the result in Studio, not in a competitor's estimated earnings.

This illustrative log is not a real channel. It exists so the subtraction can be checked. On Monday, public view totals are 20,000 for Channel North and 20,000 for Channel South. The next Monday they are 23,000 and 21,500. Channel North's observed change is 23,000 minus 20,000, which is 3,000. Channel South's observed change is 21,500 minus 20,000, which is 1,500. The North change is twice the South change, because 3,000 is 2 times 1,500. That is the entire finding. It does not say the views arrived on Wednesday, and it does not say a thumbnail caused them. If a tool's chart disagrees with these endpoint totals, record the tool's timestamp. Do not average the two stories into a third number you never observed.

## What should you ask before you keep a tracker?

Ask whether the YouTube tracker is showing your authorized report, a public observation, or someone else's model, and write the answer next to the chart. A tool earns a place in the weekly habit only if you can answer the list below without guessing.

- Is this figure from Studio, from a public page, or from an estimate?
- When was this channel or video last checked?
- Is the chart a running total or a change during a period?
- How far back does the stored history go, and do you have access to that history?
- Are Shorts separated from long-form videos?
- Do exports or alerts require access you do not have? Check the vendor's current page rather than an old screenshot.
- Are you about to compare numbers taken under different view definitions, including across August 24, 2026?
- Have estimated earnings been kept out of the same sentence as verified revenue?

If the honest answer is that you need private watch time for a channel you do not manage, stop. No tracker on this list provides it. If the honest answer is that you need to see what public Shorts in a niche look like, use a research tool. Then measure your own test in Studio. The tool is fit for the question when the evidence and the decision are the same kind of fact.

Start Studio for the channel you manage. Use Social Blade or vidIQ's public stats when you need public scale. Use a research product when the job is choosing videos to study. Keep ContHunt on the creative side of that line. A tracker is only as good as the metric name you are willing to read aloud next to the number.
