A YouTube SEO tool is any product that helps a title, a keyword, or a piece of packaging get found on YouTube. YouTube Studio is the free default, because search demand, impressions, and retention for your own channel already live there. vidIQ and TubeBuddy are browser overlays for keywords, tags, ideas, bulk edits, and packaging tests. ContHunt is not one of those overlays: it searches public TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts when the question is what to publish, not which tag to type. Public channel graphs such as Social Blade answer a different question again, subscriber and view history, and that question is not SEO. Choose the job first. A stack that pays two overlays to do one job does not make a video easier to find.

This page is the wider comparison. If you already know you are trying to replace vidIQ specifically, use [vidIQ alternatives](/blog/vidiq-alternatives) and do not treat the two articles as copies of each other.

## What a YouTube SEO tool is for

YouTube SEO, in the narrow sense, is packaging and language that help the right viewer recognize a video in search and in other surfaces where a title or thumbnail is doing the asking. A YouTube SEO tool assists that work. It might suggest phrases, score a title, bulk-edit tags, or show you search terms you already rank for. Those are assists. They are not distribution.

Search is only one way a video is found. The Shorts feed does not wait for a typed query. A tool that only understands keyword volume will look confident and still miss why a Short was swiped away. Keep the phrase "YouTube SEO tool" tied to findability. When the real problem is "what should we film," you have left SEO and entered research. When the real problem is "how many subscribers does that channel show in public," you have entered chart-reading. Naming the problem stops you buying the wrong row in the table below.

No tool in this category makes an unrelated phrase relevant, and no tool repairs a title that describes a different video from the one you uploaded. If you need the character-level rules for titles, tags, and hashtags, they live in [tags versus hashtags](/blog/youtube-tags-vs-hashtags). This page is about which product does which job.

## A comparison by job

Read the rows as jobs. "Shorts-aware" means the product can say something useful about short-form video, not that it secretly receives YouTube's feed ranking. "Free starting point" means you can begin without a paid plan. Confirm what that starting point includes on the day you sign up. Limits change, and this page does not list prices, credits, or plan names.

ContHunt is on the list because people who search "YouTube SEO tool" sometimes want "what should I publish," not "what tag do I type." It is not a live rank tracker, and it is not a vidIQ clone. Tracking inside ContHunt is experimental, so do not buy the product as a replacement for Studio. The rank you get back from a research question is a judgment of fit, with the videos attached so you can check the judgment. It is not your click-through rate.

## Start in YouTube Studio every time

YouTube Studio is the YouTube SEO tool you already have. Open [YouTube Studio](https://studio.youtube.com/) before you open a trial. First-party search and retention beat a guessed keyword list, because they describe viewers YouTube has already connected to your channel. A third-party volume number can be a useful prompt. It cannot tell you that your last ten videos on that phrase lost people in the first moments, or that the thumbnail was shown and not clicked.

A packaging pass in Studio has a fixed order:

1. Read the search terms that already bring viewers to this channel. Those phrases are evidence. A brainstormed synonym is a hypothesis.
2. Read impressions and click-through for the videos whose packaging you might change. Studio's impression and click-through data beat a new keyword subscription when the video is being shown and not chosen.
3. Read retention, or the Shorts cards that show whether people stayed, to see whether the video delivered the title. A high click-through into a fast exit is a promise you did not keep.
4. Change one of those layers on the next upload or the next test. Changing the title, the thumbnail, and the topic in the same edit makes the next report unreadable.

The Research tab is where you go when you need language, not when you need a verdict. Use it to find phrases and neighboring questions. Then look at a real video that already answers that phrase and ask whether yours is genuinely about the same thing. Keyword tools fail creators who paste a high-volume phrase onto a video that answers a different question. YouTube's viewers notice. The retention graph is how you notice them noticing.

Studio is weak at the jobs in the other rows. It will not bulk-rewrite a catalog. It will not sit on a watch page and score a draft title as you type. It will not gather public TikToks. That weakness is fine. A default tool is allowed to be incomplete. It is not allowed to be skipped.

## Use one overlay, not a pair of them

vidIQ and TubeBuddy are both browser overlays for people who work on YouTube pages all day. vidIQ leans toward keywords, ideas, and an on-page SEO score. TubeBuddy leans toward tags, bulk edits, and thumbnail tests. Both have a free place to start. Neither one is a full picture of Shorts distribution, which is why the table says "partial."

Do not buy both on day one. The overlap is the keywords-and-packaging job. A second subscription does not create a second YouTube. Pick the overlay that matches the hour you are trying to shorten. If that hour is "I need ideas and title wording inside the watch page," start with vidIQ. If that hour is "I need to update a library and run thumbnail tests," start with TubeBuddy. The bake-off for someone who specifically wants to leave vidIQ is the [vidIQ alternatives](/blog/vidiq-alternatives) page, including when the honest answer is to stay.

Treat an on-page SEO score as a heuristic. It can remind you that a title is vague or that a field is empty. It cannot see whether the video is good, whether the viewer who searches that phrase is your viewer, or whether the Shorts feed will test the upload. Creators get into trouble when they edit the video toward the score and away from the footage. If the score and the video disagree, believe the video, then make the title tell the truth.

Phrase-list tools, including keyword explorers that are really a search box with export, are a narrower product again. They hand you strings. They are weak on Shorts because a Short's problem is often the opening frame, which a string does not contain. A limited free tier is enough to see whether you even needed a list. If Studio Research already produced the phrases you will use, stop.

## When the SEO question is really "what should we publish?"

Some teams type "YouTube SEO tool" when they are stuck for subjects. A keyword list gives them more subjects of the same kind they are already bored of. Example research gives them videos. ContHunt's job on this stack is the second one: find public short-form videos on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts that already attempt the topic, and keep those videos as the evidence for a pattern.

[How Deep Search works](/resources/how-deep-search-works) is how you do that at a volume you will not watch by hand. It covers 500 to 1,000 videos in under five minutes and returns a ranked answer with the videos attached. Ask a publishing question, not a tag question. "Which openings are public creators using for a charger that will not stay in the port?" is a publishing question. "Best tags for tech channel" is a tag question, and it belongs in Studio or an overlay, where it will still not film the video.

Use the examples to write one test, then go back to Studio to see whether your version was found and watched. Public likes on someone else's Short are not your future click-through rate. If the sales page you are comparing only shows a viral score, skip it. A score without the videos is a mood.

## Subscriber graphs are not a YouTube SEO tool

Social Blade and similar public graphs show how a channel's public totals move. That can be useful context for a partnership or a competitive set. It does not tell you which title to write, which phrase your audience searches, or which second your viewers leave. Calling a graph an SEO tool is how teams buy a chart and still ship a vague title.

If you need that public history, use a tracker and keep it out of the packaging workflow. [YouTube tracker tools](/blog/youtube-tracker) cover that job. Studio remains the place where your own subscriber gains are tied to a video. A public graph of a competitor cannot show whether their last upload earned subscribers or only views, unless they have published that breakdown themselves.

## What no YouTube SEO tool can do

Say these limits out loud before a renewal.

- It cannot force the recommendation system to keep showing a Short people swipe away from. Findability is not the same as satisfaction.
- It cannot make an off-topic tag relevant. Language that the video never supports does not become metadata. It becomes a mismatch.
- It cannot replace a title that promises a different video. Viewers who click and leave teach the system, and your retention report, that the promise failed.
- It cannot show a competitor's private search terms, impressions, or retention. Connected-account features show accounts that have been connected. Public tools show public objects.
- It cannot decide between two strategic topics. A higher search volume on a phrase you cannot demonstrate is a trap, not an opportunity.

If the problem is packaging, fix packaging and read Studio. If the problem is ignorance of which topics already work in the niche, use example research. If the problem is a desire for a score on every upload, use one overlay and remember the score is a checklist with a number on it.

## A default stack

The default is short because every extra login has to delete a duplicate job.

1. Studio, always, for search terms, impressions, click-through, retention, and audience.
2. One overlay, vidIQ or TubeBuddy, if and only if you work on YouTube pages in a way Studio makes clumsy.
3. ContHunt, when you also publish Shorts, Reels, or TikTok and the next video needs public examples rather than another phrase list.
4. The tag and hashtag rules in [tags versus hashtags](/blog/youtube-tags-vs-hashtags), so the overlay does not talk you into stuffing a description.

Drop a layer the moment it duplicates a paid job. Two phrase lists are a hobby. One phrase list plus a retention review is a workflow.

## How to judge a new YouTube SEO tool

Ask four questions before another subscription. Write the answers down. A maybe is a no until the trial proves otherwise.

1. Does it show YouTube data you cannot already see in Studio? If it only redraws your search terms, you are paying for a theme.
2. Does it work on Shorts, or only on long-form search? A long-form keyword habit applied to a feed video produces titles that sound like blog posts and openings that explain themselves too late.
3. Is it an overlay, a keyword list, or a research feed? Those are different products. An overlay sits on YouTube. A list exports strings. A research feed returns videos. A vendor who blurs the three in one sentence has not picked a job.
4. Can you leave in 30 days if click-through and view duration do not move? A 30-day window is long enough to ship a few packaged videos and short enough that a bad renewal does not become a quarter. If the contract makes leaving harder than learning, do not start.

During those 30 days, change one packaging variable and leave the footage's promise alone, or change the footage's promise and leave the packaging vocabulary alone. An illustrative pair, not a result: "My morning routine" versus "The morning reset I use when I sleep badly." The second title tells you the niche and the situation. Neither title is a proven click-through rate. You find out which one your channel's viewers choose by reading impressions and click-through after the upload, not by asking the tool that suggested the words to grade its own homework.

If the sales page leads with a viral score and will not say whether the product is an overlay, a list, or a feed, skip it. Scores that cannot point at a video, a search term, or a Studio card are not SEO. They are decoration.
