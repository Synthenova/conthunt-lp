YouTube tags vs hashtags is a field split, not two names for one box. Written by the ContHunt team. Tags catch misspellings. Hashtags are labels people can click. A hashtag is a word or phrase with a # in the title or the description. YouTube's hashtag help says hashtags connect a video with other videos and playlists that share the label. Viewers can open a results page from the label. A tag is a keyword in the separate Tags box in YouTube Studio. Viewers do not see that box, and YouTube's tag help says the title, thumbnail, and description matter more for discovery. Tags help mainly when people misspell the topic. Otherwise they play a minimal role. For a Short, write a title a person can read. Add a few hashtags that name the video. Use the tags field only for real synonyms and misspellings.

## Are YouTube tags and hashtags the same?

YouTube tags and YouTube hashtags are not the same field, and typing the same words in both places does not stack a ranking. Hashtags are visible. Tags are hidden metadata. The confusion is understandable because both are words about the topic, and older upload advice treated every box as a place to repeat a keyword. YouTube's current help pages split the jobs.

[Add tags to your YouTube videos](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/146402) says tags are descriptive keywords you can add so viewers can find the content, then immediately qualifies that. The title, thumbnail, and description are more important pieces of metadata. Those are what viewers use to decide what to watch. Tags can be useful if the content is commonly misspelled. Otherwise, tags play a minimal role in discovery. The same help page says that adding excessive tags to the description is against YouTube's spam, deceptive practices, and scams policies. That warning is about the description, not a license to fill the hidden Tags box either.

[Find playlists and videos using hashtags](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6390658) defines a hashtag as a keyword preceded by a # symbol. Hashtags let you connect the upload with other videos or playlists that share the hashtag on YouTube and YouTube Music. They let viewers find related content through search. Hashtags in the title and in the description link to a results page of other videos that share the label.

If you only have time for one pass on a Short, spend it on the title and on accurate hashtags. The tags field is a backup for spellings, not the packaging.

## What does each field do?

A YouTube hashtag is a public label. A YouTube tag is a private hint for a misspelling or a close variant. This is the split to keep while you upload.

| | Hashtags | Tags field |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where you type them | Title or description, each one starting with # | Studio Tags box, words only, no # |
| Whether viewers see them | Yes. They can be clicked | No. Viewers do not get a tags control |
| What YouTube's help emphasizes | Connecting videos that share a topic, and search | Misspellings. Otherwise a minimal role beside title, thumbnail, and description |
| What breaks them | Unrelated labels, spaces inside a label, or more than 60 hashtags on the video | Repeating the title, or pasting hashtags with the # still attached |
| What a Short should do | A few labels that match the video | A handful of synonyms or misspellings, or nothing. |

The failure mode on the hashtag side is stricter than a style preference. YouTube's hashtag policy says that if a video or playlist has more than 60 hashtags, YouTube will ignore each hashtag on that content. The cutoff is not "the extras are dropped." Every hashtag on that upload is ignored. Over-tagging can also lead to the video being removed from uploads or from search. Sixty is allowed. Sixty-one is already more than 60. An illustrative description that adds a 61st hashtag is past the line where YouTube says it ignores them all. You do not need 61 labels to describe a Short. You need enough room under that line that a copied block from another video cannot push you over it by accident.

## How many hashtags does YouTube show?

YouTube may show up to three hashtags by the video title, chosen from the hashtags in the description. It keeps the rest of a legal set in the description. The help page says this. Out of all the hashtags you add to the description, up to three that are considered most engaging appear by the title. The hashtags still appear in the description, and the video can still appear in search results. Hashtags placed in the title also link to a results page. The "up to three" line is specifically about hashtags in the description, and YouTube picks which ones it considers most engaging. It does not promise to display the first three you typed. It does not promise to display hashtags that live only in the title in those slots.

A working default for a Short is three to five hashtags that match the video. That number is a publishing habit, not a quota YouTube prints as use five. Three to five stays far under the 60-hashtag line. It gives the description a small set from which YouTube can choose up to three to place by the title. Adding thirty copies of the same label does not create thirty chances. The policy problem with a huge set is the opposite of a boost.

Where the hashtag sits is a separate choice from whether you use one. The [title versus description](/blog/youtube-shorts-hashtags-title-vs-description) guide is the place for that placement test. Which labels belong on a given niche is the [YouTube Shorts hashtags guide](/blog/youtube-shorts-hashtags-guide). This page is only the difference between those labels and the hidden tags field.

Hashtags also have to be hashtags, not sentences. YouTube's rules say a hashtag contains no spaces. Two words are joined, as in `#TwoWords` or `#twowords`. A space ends the label. Community Guidelines apply. Hashtags that violate policy will not show below the content and may be removed. Unrelated or misleading hashtags can get the video or playlist removed under misleading metadata rules. Hashtags used to harass, and hashtags that promote hatred or include slurs, can get the video removed. Sexual or explicit hashtags can get the video removed. Profanity in a hashtag can lead to an age restriction or removal. Those are policy outcomes, not ranking tips. A label that does not describe the video is the wrong label even before those rules.

One more description rule sits next to the tags conversation. YouTube says that adding ordinary descriptive tags or repetitive sentences in the description is still prohibited. Violating it can remove or penalize the video or playlist. The hidden Tags box is the place YouTube provides for extra keywords. The description is not a second tags dump. A sentence that tells a viewer what the Short covers is fine. A paragraph of comma-separated keywords is the pattern the policy is written against.

## What do you type in each field?

Type a readable title, a few hashtags in the description, and only real variants in the tags field. The examples below are patterns for a video about this topic. They are not a claim that those phrases have a known search volume.

Title:

```text
Why my Shorts stopped using 12 tags
```

The "12" in that title is part of the sentence, not a recommended tag count. YouTube's tag help does not publish a required number of tags. It says extra tags matter less than the title, the thumbnail, and the description, except when they catch a misspelling.

Description hashtags follow.

```text
#shorts #youtubetags #videoseo
```

That is three labels. It matches the "up to three by the title" display cap if YouTube chooses to surface all of them. It does not guarantee those three are the ones marked most engaging. A fourth or fifth label that also names the video is consistent with the three-to-five habit. A block of unrelated trend names is not.

The tags field is comma-separated, with no # character.

```text
youtube tags, youtube hashtags, shorts seo, you tube tags.
```

`you tube tags` is in that list because it is a spacing a searcher might type. That is the misspelling case YouTube's help actually describes. Do not put `#shorts` in the tags field. The # is not part of that field. Pasting it is mostly pointless. It does not turn the hidden field into a hashtag, and it does not add a clickable label.

For a Short about how to add captions, a relevant tag set might include `how to add captions to shorts` and `shorts captions`. Add a common misspelling of captions only if you have actually seen people use it. Keep only terms that describe the video. A generator's suggestion is not evidence of search volume. If you cannot explain why a tag is a synonym or a misspelling of what the Short shows, leave it out.

YouTube documents two places to enter tags. For a new upload, open YouTube Studio, start the upload, and add tags from More options on the details step. For a video already online, open Content, select the video, and use Show more. The help page also says tags can be edited from the YouTube app on mobile. None of those steps ask you to paste the description's hashtags into the box.

## Do Shorts and long-form use the fields differently?

YouTube Shorts and long-form videos use the same tags field and the same hashtag rules. The difference is how much of the viewer's decision happens before search. A Short is often met as motion. The opening, the title, and whether people keep watching do more of that introduction than a hidden keyword list. Long-form browsing and search still reward a title that names the video clearly, and the tags field can cover a misspelling there too. That is not a published weight that says tags are worth a set percentage more on long videos. YouTube's help does not give that percentage. It says the same thing for the videos you tag: minimal role, except misspellings, behind title, thumbnail, and description.

So a Short should not inherit a long keyword ritual. Three to five accurate hashtags and a handful of real variants are enough metadata after the title is written. Rebuilding a stuffed list for a short vertical video spends the upload time on the field YouTube has said matters least. If the spoken words, the on-screen text, and the title disagree about the topic, no tag repairs that. The viewer and the metadata are then describing different videos.

Hashtags remain worth doing on Shorts because they are the public label. YouTube's help says you can add them when you upload a video or record a Short. When you add text to a Short and start a hashtag, YouTube can suggest labels. Those suggestions draw on signals such as past uploads and, in some countries, the text you entered. A clock icon marks a hashtag you have used before. A suggestion is a memory and a recommendation, not a count of how often the phrase is searched. Treat it the way you treat any other autocomplete: keep it if it names this video, drop it if it is only familiar.

## What is a sensible order at publish time?

Publish a Short by locking the title first, then the description, then hashtags, then tags. The order stops the hidden field from eating the time the visible fields need.

1. Write the title as a normal sentence or a clear phrase. The promise should be readable without a string of labels. The [title character limit](/blog/youtube-title-character-limit-2026) is the constraint on how long that line can be. Hashtags you put in the title consume that same budget, so a title full of labels is a title people cannot scan.
2. Add one or two sentences of description that say what the Short shows. Do not paste a keyword paragraph. YouTube still treats repetitive keyword sentences in the description as a policy problem.
3. Add three to five hashtags that match the footage. Put them where you have decided they belong, in the title or the description. The up-to-three behavior is described for description hashtags.
4. Open the tags field only if you have a misspelling or a tight synonym. A handful of those is the whole job. Leave the field sparse if you have none.
5. Read the title aloud against the first spoken line. If they name different topics, fix the video or the title before you add another tag.

## Which mix-ups are worth unlearning?

The mix-ups around YouTube tags and hashtags are mechanical, and each one has a direct fix.

- Pasting hashtags into the tags field, # included, does not create a clickable label. Remove the # and keep the word only if it is a real variant. Otherwise delete it.
- Pasting a tag list into the title makes the title unreadable and spends the title's character budget on words viewers experience as clutter. The title is the sentence. The hashtags are the labels.
- Duplicating the same phrase in the title, the description, the hashtags, and the tags field does not multiply distribution. YouTube has already said the visible metadata does the discovery work, and that more than 60 hashtags causes every hashtag to be ignored.
- Treating a suggestion or a generator list as proof of demand will fill both fields with phrases the video never says. Check any candidate against the footage and, when you have it, against search terms YouTube reports for your own channel. Another tool's list is not that report.
- Using a hashtag because it is popular on a different video, when this Short is about something else, is the misleading-metadata case. The label has to be about this upload.
- Putting a space in a hashtag splits it. `#you tube` is not one label. Join the words or drop one of them.

YouTube tags versus hashtags comes down to visibility and to what the help pages assign each field. Hashtags are the public labels, with a display cap of up to three from the description and a hard stop at more than 60. Tags are the quiet misspellings. The title is still the line a person reads before they decide the Short is the one they meant to open.

Before the next upload, write the title in plain language and add only hashtags you could defend from the footage. Look at public Shorts in the same niche and copy the clarity of their titles. [Get started]({{GET_STARTED_URL}}) there, not from the length of anyone's hidden tag list.
