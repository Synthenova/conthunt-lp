## The outlier rubric that avoids lucky flukes (Median-of-10 + 3-Channel Check)

Most beginners waste hours chasing the wrong "outliers" because they compare views to subscriber count or channel average. Two practical rules cut the noise immediately — whether you use 1of10, ViewStats, ContHunt, or a free spreadsheet:

1. **Use the MEDIAN of the last 10 uploads, never the average or subscriber count.** Plenty of 50,000-subscriber channels average 800 views per upload, and plenty of 3,000-subscriber channels average 15,000. On Shorts especially, a single 200,000-view breakout drags a 10-video mean from 2,000 up to 22,000 and hides real winners. A practical bookmark line is **5x the channel's recent 10-upload median** on an upload under 90 days old, and **10x+ median** for a strong breakout.
2. **Run the 3-channel cross-validation check before scripting.** Before you commit to a topic, check whether the same underlying angle is outperforming on **2–3 unrelated small channels**, while **not yet covered by the top 5 giant channels** in your niche. If only one small channel blew up, it is often a personality quirk or clickbait fluke. If 20 channels already copied the exact angle this week, the topic is already saturated.

## Running multiple Shorts channels? Keep public research read-only

Creators managing 3–5+ YouTube Shorts channels often hesitate to connect one third-party app with full YouTube Studio OAuth permissions across every channel. You do not need to link private Studio logins to do competitor and outlier research:

- **Keep competitor and idea tracking 100% public and read-only:** Log five columns per test or reference video — `Channel`, `Hook Type`, `Format`, `48-hour Public Views`, and `Ratio vs. 10-Video Median`.
- **Keep private retention inside each channel's own YouTube Studio tab:** Check Swipe-vs-Viewed, Average Percentage Viewed, and Studio Trends ("Videos your audience watches") directly inside YouTube without granting unnecessary cross-channel OAuth access.
