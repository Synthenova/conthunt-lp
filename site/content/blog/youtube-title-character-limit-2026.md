The YouTube title character limit is 100 characters. Written by the ContHunt team. YouTube's help page on editing video details states that video titles have a character limit of 100 characters and cannot include invalid characters. One hundred is the hard stop in the details field, not a target to hit. A practical length for most videos is 45 to 70 characters, with the main promise written so it is already clear in the first 40. That 45 to 70 range, and the first-40 target, are packaging habits for phone previews, search, and Shorts surfaces. They are not a second limit YouTube publishes beside the 100-character cap. A Short uses a title too. The title will not carry the whole video, but it is how search, a channel page, and related surfaces learn the topic before anyone watches.

## What is the YouTube title character limit?

The maximum length of a YouTube title is 100 characters. [Create and edit video details](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/57404) says so in the table of video settings. The title has a character limit of 100 characters, and invalid characters are not allowed. YouTube does not publish, on that page, the list of characters it rejects. If the field refuses a symbol, remove the symbol rather than hunting for an unofficial list of banned marks.

The same page sets the description limit at 5,000 characters. The description is the long field. The title is the short one. Creators who plan a paragraph and then discover the title stopped accepting keystrokes are running into the 100-character line, not a bug in the description. Hashtags you type in the title are characters in that same 100. They are not a separate budget. A title that spends its last characters on labels has less room for the promise. Where those labels belong, title or description, is covered in [YouTube tags versus hashtags](/blog/youtube-tags-vs-hashtags).

Use the full 100 only for an edge case the video cannot name in fewer characters. A comparison that needs both objects and a qualifier is one case. A title that consumes all 100 characters often hides the promise, repeats one keyword, or arrives on a phone already cut off. The limit is permission, not a goal. Stopping at 99 instead of 100 does not make a vague title specific.

Count characters, not words. "YouTube Shorts" is 14 characters plus the ideas around it. A title can feel short when you say it and still be long once the platform name, the colon, and a year are included. Count the line in the upload field, or count the exact string you are about to paste, including spaces and punctuation. Spaces count. A trailing space counts. An emoji, if the field accepts it, counts as the characters it uses, and it still has to survive the invalid-character rule.

## What length should you write in practice?

A practical YouTube title length is 45 to 70 characters. That band is wide enough for a topic and a reason to watch. It is short enough that the line can finish before a small preview gives up. It is a writing target. YouTube will accept a title of 20 characters and a title of 100 characters. Clarity is the constraint the character counter will not enforce.

Write shorter than 45 when the promise is already obvious from a simple keyword. Also write shorter when the video is a Short with a visual that carries the context, or when the cover already shows the result. Write toward the long end of the band, or past it but still under 100, when the title has to distinguish similar videos. The qualifier can be a comparison, a year, a niche, or a use case. A longer title is justified by a real qualifier, not by a second copy of the same noun.

The first 40 characters are the part to protect. They are the most likely to remain when a mobile feed, a Shorts surface, or a search preview truncates the line. Forty is not a published truncation point. YouTube's help page does not say that titles are cut at 40. The habit exists because small layouts cut somewhere. The words at the start are the ones a layout has to show if it shows anything. Put the platform, the topic, and the outcome in that opening. Put the year, the niche, and the parenthetical angle after the promise is already complete.

This illustrative title is 65 characters, which sits inside 45 to 70:

```text
YouTube Shorts: put the promise first. Year and niche can follow.
```

The first 40 characters of that string are `YouTube Shorts: put the promise first. Y`. The completed promise, `YouTube Shorts: put the promise first.`, is 38 characters. It fits before the cutoff, and the 40th character is only the start of the word Year. The remaining 25 characters, `ear and niche can follow.`, are optional context. A viewer who sees a shortened line still gets the promise. A title that puts Year and niche first and the promise last does the opposite. The shortened line is the context, and the point is what got cut. Sixty-five minus 40 is 25, which is the length of that leftover. The arithmetic is a check on this example, not a measurement of a live preview.

## Which titles are clearer, not just shorter?

A YouTube title inside the practical range can still be a weak title. Length is the container. Specificity is the content. The pairs below are packaging examples. The numbers in them, such as seven fixes or nine fixes, are part of the example phrasing. They are not a counted study of videos. Character counts include spaces and punctuation.

| Weaker title | Characters | Clearer title | Characters |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| My Full Guide to Everything I Learned About YouTube Shorts This Year | 68 | YouTube Shorts Hooks: 7 Fixes for Weak Retention | 48 |
| Important Instagram Reels Tips You Need to Know in 2026 | 55 | Instagram Reels Best Practices: 9 Growth Fixes | 46 |
| How I Use Analytics Tools for Videos | 36 | YouTube Shorts Analytics: What to Check First | 45. |

Read the counts before you draw a rule from the left column. The first weaker title is 68 characters, which is inside 45 to 70. It is still weak because everything I learned and this year delay the topic. The second weaker title is 55 characters, also inside the band, and it says "tips you need to know" without a tip. The third weaker title is 36 characters, under the band. It is vague in a different way. Analytics tools and videos could be any workflow. The clearer lines name an object and a payoff. Hooks and retention. Best practices and a growth fix. Analytics and what to check first. Two of those clearer lines, 48 and 46, sit in the band. The analytics line is 45, on the floor of the band. The short vague line at 36 shows that dropping under 45 does not create clarity. The long vague line at 68 shows that landing inside the band does not create it either.

A structure that fits the limit has three beats, and the third is optional:

1. The primary promise or the keyword, early enough to survive a short preview.
2. The specific outcome, so the title is not only a topic tag.
3. Context, such as a year or a niche, only if two videos would otherwise share a title.

"YouTube Shorts Hooks: 7 Fixes for Weak Retention" follows that order. The topic is in the first words. The outcome is retention, with a concrete kind of fix. Nothing after that is required. Adding the words in 2026 for beginners who struggle would spend characters without a new distinction. That extra text is useful only when the video is actually limited to that year and that audience.

## How should a Shorts title differ from a long-form title?

A YouTube Shorts title should usually be more direct than a long-form title. The line is scanned on a phone next to a vertical cover. The hard cap does not change. One hundred characters is still 100 characters. What changes is how much of that allowance is worth using. A Short that shows one mistake does not need a title that previews an entire curriculum.

Direct Shorts titles share a few habits.

- Lead with the topic, not with I or you need to.
- Avoid vague curiosity that could sit on any video. "Wait for it" does not name a subject.
- Keep the payoff visible in the words that will not be cut.
- Use the same language as the hook. If the first spoken line says "captions," the title should not say "subtitles" unless you have a reason to treat them as different.
- Skip filler such as "you need to watch this." Those words cost characters and add no topic.

Patterns that stay inside a short scan, with their character counts:

| 3 YouTube Shorts Hook Mistakes | 30 | Topic and format are both in the line, under the 45-character side of the band because the idea is simple |
| Best Length for YouTube Shorts | 30 | The query and the object are the whole title |
| Instagram Reels Caption Fix | 27 | One problem, one surface |
| Why This Thumbnail Gets Clicked | 31 | A specific packaging question, not a generic "tips" line. |

A Shorts title near 30 characters is a legitimate length when the visual is doing the rest. The four lines above are 30, 30, 27, and 31 characters. None of those counts is a new maximum. A Short that compares two tools, or that only makes sense with a year in the title, can move into the 45 to 70 band. The test is whether a stranger can predict the first sentence of the video from the title. If the title promises a data-backed answer, the video has to show the evidence quickly. A precise title on a rambling Short trains people to leave.

Front-load the words that identify the video. Platform words such as YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, or TikTok belong early when the viewer might be choosing among platforms. Topic words such as hashtags, thumbnails, analytics, or competitor analysis belong early because they are the search. Outcome words, such as a clearer cover or a faster check, belong as soon as the topic is named. Year, niche, and a secondary angle wait. A title that starts with "2026" spends the opening on a number every competing video can also use.

The cover has to be able to say the same thing in a small image. [YouTube Shorts thumbnail best practices](/blog/youtube-shorts-thumbnail-best-practices-2026) is the visual half of this decision. A 70-character title cannot rescue a cover that shows an unrelated reaction. A perfect cover cannot rescue a title whose first 40 characters are throat-clearing.

## YouTube Shorts title best practices

A YouTube Shorts title works when it names one concrete idea in the first words and stays well under the 100-character cap. These are the same rules as the length section above, applied to a line that is scanned on a phone next to a vertical cover.

- Lead with the topic, not with "I" or "you need to."
- Put a finished promise inside the first 40 characters.
- Stay nearer 30 characters when the cover already shows the subject. Move toward 45 to 70 only when a year or a comparison is required for the line to be true.
- Match the spoken hook. If the first line says "captions," the title should not say "subtitles" unless you mean a different thing.
- Leave hashtags out of the title when they push the promise past the opening. The description is the place for them.
- Do not spend the spare characters under 100 on a second copy of the keyword, a channel name, or an episode number.

## How do you check a title before you upload?

Check a YouTube title by counting to 100, reading the first 40 characters alone, and matching the line to the video you actually cut. Do this in the upload field so the count is the field's count, not a guess from a notes app that treats characters differently.

- The title is 100 characters or fewer, and it contains no character the field rejects.
- The main promise is understandable from the first 40 characters, read without the rest of the line.
- Most titles land between 45 and 70 characters, unless a shorter line is already specific or a qualifier genuinely needs the extra room under 100.
- The title and the thumbnail or cover promise the same result.
- The video delivers that result. A title is not a place to claim a study, a rank, or a number the footage does not show.
- A viewer on a phone can tell what the video is in a single glance. If the line only makes sense after a second clause, move that clause's meaning forward.
- Hashtags in the title, if you use them, are included in the count. If they push the promise past the opening, move the labels to the description.
- The description, which can run to 5,000 characters on the same help page, is where a real sentence of context belongs. Do not try to store that sentence in the title by abbreviating it into fragments.

An illustrative rewrite shows the check without pretending it is a channel result. Start with a 68-character line that is technically legal and still late.

```text
My Full Guide to Everything I Learned About YouTube Shorts This Year
```

The opening 40 are `My Full Guide to Everything I Learned Ab`. The leftover is `out YouTube Shorts This Year`, which is 28 characters, and 40 plus 28 is 68. The word "About" is split, and "YouTube Shorts" sits entirely in the leftover. A shortened preview is a guide to "everything," which is not a topic. The clearer 48-character line puts the topic first.

```text
YouTube Shorts Hooks: 7 Fixes for Weak Retention
```

The first 40 characters of that line are `YouTube Shorts Hooks: 7 Fixes for Weak R`. The leftover is `etention`, which is 8 characters, and 40 plus 8 is 48. "YouTube Shorts Hooks" is inside the opening slice. The outcome word "Retention" is not finished inside those 40 characters, because the slice keeps the R and leaves eight letters. That is still a better opening than everything I learned. It is a reminder to finish the payoff word before the slice ends if that word has to be visible. Do not publish a title whose first 40 characters are "My full guide to everything."

Apply the same check when you are looking at competitors. A title that keeps showing up on search is a public sentence you can read. It is not a measurement of that creator's click-through rate. Save the pattern, not a superstition about one exact length. "Names the object, then the payoff, under 70 characters" is a pattern. Hitting one magic character count is not.

[YouTube Shorts best practices](/blog/youtube-shorts-best-practices) is the wider packaging context once the line is the right length. Title length will not fix a hook that never states the promise the title just made. Read the title and the first spoken line together. If they could be about two different videos, one of them is wrong.

## What is the YouTube tag character limit?

The YouTube tag character limit is not the 100-character title limit on this page. Tags are a separate field. YouTube's help page on adding tags, checked on 9 November 2026, says tags are descriptive keywords, that the title, thumbnail, and description matter more for discovery, and that tags help mainly when the topic is commonly misspelled. That page does not publish a character cap. Do not spend the title's 100 characters repeating a tag.

## What should you stop doing with the extra characters?

Stop using the spare room under 100 characters as a storage bin. The characters you do not need are not a waste. They are what keeps the promise inside a preview. Common ways to spend them badly:

- Repeating the keyword in singular and plural back to back. Viewers do not need the noun twice, and the tags field, not the title, is where YouTube says a misspelling belongs.
- Stacking a year, a vertical bar, a niche, and an audience onto a title that was already clear at 50 characters.
- Leading with a channel name or "episode 14" so the topic starts after the preview's likely cutoff.
- Copying a search phrase that includes words you will not say in the video. The title then collects clicks the footage cannot satisfy.
- Writing the title to hit 70 exactly. The top of the practical band is not a score.

If two of your videos would have the same title, add the smallest qualifier that separates them, and count again. A year is a fair qualifier when the video is about what changed that year. It is clutter when it is only there because the calendar turned. A niche is a fair qualifier when the advice would be wrong for a different niche. It is clutter when it is a tag you also put in the hashtags.

The YouTube title character limit in 2026 is 100 characters, including for the details field you use when you edit a video. Write most titles between 45 and 70 characters. Put a finished promise inside the first 40. Let a Short be even more direct when one concrete idea is the whole video. Then make the cover and the opening say that same idea.
