Finding a winning product from short-form signals means scoring creator spread, platform spread, velocity, demo clarity, comment intent, objections and saturation before you spend on samples. One viral video proves one video. A product worth sourcing shows many unrelated creators, buyers using the same words in the comments, a benefit you can see in five seconds, and recent uploads still outperforming.

## What short-form can tell you, and what it cannot

Short-form video can support a product decision on demand language, demonstration clarity, creator spread and timing, and it cannot tell you margin, supplier quality, return rate, conversion or seasonality. Treat the scorecard as a filter that decides what deserves a supplier quote, not as a sales forecast.

## The seven-signal scorecard

The seven-signal scorecard gives each signal 0, 1 or 2 points, and the total decides shortlist, watch list or skip. The thresholds below are starting points, so adjust them to the size of your category.

| Signal | 2 points | 1 point | 0 points |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Creator spread | 5+ unrelated creators posted it in the last 14 days | 2 to 4 creators | One creator or a single network of creators |
| Platform spread | Appears on TikTok and on Reels or Shorts | Appears on two platforms with few videos | One platform only |
| Velocity | Recent videos beat each creator's own median | Mixed | Recent videos below median |
| Demo clarity | Benefit is visible in 5 seconds, no sound needed | Needs a voiceover to land | Benefit cannot be shown |
| Comment intent | Many comments ask where to buy, price, or fit | Some | Mostly jokes and tags |
| Objections | Top objections can be answered with a feature or a guarantee | Some can | Objections are about safety, legality or durability |
| Saturation | Few sellers, varied ad creative | Several sellers | Many sellers using the same creative |

A total of 10 or more goes on the shortlist. 7 to 9 goes on a watch list for next week. Below 7 is a skip.

## How to collect each signal

Collecting each signal means counting distinct creators, comparing a video with its creator's median, muting the demo, sampling comments and checking public ad libraries for the same item. Search the buyer's words, not only the product name.

**Creator spread and platform spread.** Search the product's category words and the buyer's problem words, not just the product name. Many items circulate without a stable name. Count distinct creators, then check whether the videos show the same SKU. Look at how each video was made, too. Ten near-identical videos from creators in one agency's roster count as one source.

**Velocity.** For each video, divide views by the creator's median views on recent posts. A ratio above 2 in the last two weeks suggests the product, not the creator's audience, did the work. Daily snapshots show whether it is still rising. Compare day-3 views across videos posted a week apart.

**Demo clarity.** Mute ten videos. If you can still say what the product does, it passes. Products that need a story to make sense are expensive to advertise.

**Comment intent.** Sample around 100 comments on each of five videos and tally them:

| Comment type | Example | What it tells you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchase intent | "link?", "where do I get this" | Demand exists and the path is unclear |
| Price | "how much", "is it worth it" | Price sensitivity band |
| Fit | "does it work with iPhone 15?", "what size" | Product questions your page must answer |
| Skepticism | "does this really work?" | Proof beats you need in the ad |
| Social | "tag your friend" | Awareness and shareability, weaker buying intent |
| Joke | emojis, memes | Noise |

A product with 20% or more purchase intent and fit comments is easier to sell than one with the same views and mostly social comments. Those cutoffs are working rules, so calibrate them against products you have sold.

**Saturation.** Check the public ad libraries for the same product. Meta runs an [Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/) and TikTok's [Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/) lists top ads. Count active advertisers selling the exact item, and compare their creative. If twenty sellers run the same unboxing, you will need a better angle than the product.

## Worked example (illustrative)

The worked example scores a made-up magnetic cable organizer so you can see how seven observations become one total. These numbers are invented to show the method.

| Signal | Observation | Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Creator spread | 9 unrelated desk-setup creators in 14 days | 2 |
| Platform spread | TikTok plus a few Reels, no Shorts | 1 |
| Velocity | 7 of 9 videos above creator median | 2 |
| Demo clarity | Cables snap into place, visible with no audio | 2 |
| Comment intent | About 12% buying or fit questions | 1 |
| Objections | Mostly "does it hold heavy cables" (testable) | 1 |
| Saturation | 14 advertisers, same unboxing creative | 0 |
| Total | | 9 of 14 |

At 9, it goes on the watch list. The demand evidence is strong and the saturation score is the problem. The plan is to order a sample, test whether it holds a charging cable for a laptop, and look for an angle the 14 advertisers are not using, such as a travel kit or a cable-per-device bundle. If the angle fails, the product waits.

## The kill list

The kill list names products to skip or investigate before sourcing, even when the seven-signal total looks strong. A high score does not override claim risk, someone else's IP or a restricted category.

Skip or investigate further before sourcing when any of these apply:

- **Health and skin claims.** Products that sell through "cures", "detox" or medical outcomes carry claim risk. Videos can say things your ads cannot.
- **Other people's IP.** Branded characters, logos and lookalikes.
- **Regulated hardware.** Batteries, chargers, heaters and anything marketed to children usually need compliance work.
- **High return risk.** Size-sensitive, fragile or fit-dependent items.
- **Restricted categories.** Check the marketplace's restricted-products policy before you plan around it.
- **Fake proof.** The FTC's [final rule on fake reviews and testimonials](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) bans fake and AI-generated reviews, and buying fake social metrics. Build proof from real customers.

## Rescore the watch list one week later

A watch-list rescore repeats the same seven signals a week later, and it is how a 7 to 9 total either earns a sample or stays off the order. The bands do not move. A total of 10 or more is still the shortlist, 7 to 9 is still the watch list, and below 7 is still a skip. The illustrative cable organizer scored 9 of 14, held back by saturation at 0, so the next action was a sample, a search for an angle the existing advertisers were not using, and another look at whether velocity held.

Change a point only when the observation changed. Do not add an eighth signal because the product feels more exciting on second viewing. Write the new score in the same 0, 1 or 2 cells.

- **Creator spread.** Count distinct creators again over the last 14 days, and check that the videos still show the same SKU. Ten near-identical videos from one roster still count as one source. Moving from 2 to 4 creators into the 5+ band is a real change in this signal. A burst inside one network is not, even if the view totals look new.
- **Platform spread.** Note whether the item is still on one platform, on two platforms with few videos, or on TikTok and on Reels or Shorts. The worked example had TikTok plus a few Reels and no Shorts, which scored 1. A later appearance on Shorts can move that cell. A few more videos on the same two platforms may not.
- **Velocity.** Divide views by the creator's median again. The working line in this guide is a ratio above 2 on recent videos in the last two weeks. Also compare day-3 views across videos posted a week apart. New posts below the creator's median drop the signal even when older videos still look large. In the example, 7 of 9 videos sat above the median. If a later week reverses that mix, the 2 becomes a 1 or a 0.
- **Demo clarity.** Mute the new videos, the same way as the first pass, which mutes ten. The benefit still has to be obvious in about 5 seconds with no sound. A clearer voiceover does not raise this score. The score is about what the picture shows alone.
- **Comment intent and objections.** Sample around 100 comments on each of five videos, and tally purchase, price, fit, skepticism, social and joke. The working comparison in this guide is that 20% or more purchase-intent and fit comments is an easier product to sell than the same views with mostly social comments. The example sat at about 12%, which scored 1. Also check whether the objection you hoped to answer is still the one buyers ask. An objection about safety, legality or durability still scores 0 on that signal, whatever the other cells say.
- **Saturation.** Look again at the public ad libraries for the exact item and for whether the creative is still one shared unboxing. The example had 14 advertisers on the same creative, scored 0, against a separate caution that twenty sellers on the same unboxing means you need a better angle than the product. More advertisers on one creative is a worse saturation score, not a better one.

Put the new total beside last week's total. The gap is the decision. A product that stays inside 7 to 9 while velocity falls and saturation gets worse does not graduate because you already ordered a sample. A product that crosses 10 because creator spread and velocity rose, and that is still clear of the kill list, earns the sample test described in the next section. The scorecard still says nothing reliable about margin, supplier quality, return rate, conversion or seasonality. The rescore only tells you whether the short-form pattern is still the one you scored.

## From shortlist to test

Moving from the shortlist to a test means the scorecard has finished its job and your own numbers take over. Order samples before inventory. Shoot your own demo, and brief creators with the proof beats the comment analysis revealed. Judge the test by your own numbers: cost per purchase, return rate, refund reasons. The scorecard only earns the right to run that test.
