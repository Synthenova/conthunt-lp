Some manage comments and inboxes for accounts you own. Some benchmark competitor profiles. Some study public short-form video. ContHunt is for short-form content research across TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. It is not a full brand-listening suite, and it is not an inbox. ## What is social media monitoring, and how is it different from listening?

Social media monitoring usually means tracking mentions, comments, alerts, and visible activity. Social listening is the broader pass over themes, sentiment, narratives, and market shifts. Vendors use the two words differently, so the useful check is the data source and the limit, not the word on the sales page.

A monitoring product can be any of these, and they do not substitute for each other:

- A mention index that alerts you when a name appears on news, blogs, forums, reviews, or social posts.
- An inbox on accounts you have connected, where a person replies to comments and messages.
- A profile benchmark that compares public posts from a list of competitors.
- A content-research workflow that studies public videos for hooks, captions, and formats.

Private messages are a separate case. Some management suites include an inbox for connected accounts. That inbox requires an authorized connection to each platform. It is not the same system as public web and social listening, and a listening contract does not quietly include it.

## What do the enterprise platforms actually claim?

Enterprise tools in this category are built for coverage and governance. The claims below are the vendors' claims. They are not ContHunt measurements, and they are not a quality score.

### Sprout Social

Sprout Social is a full social management suite: publishing, engagement, analytics, listening, and team workflows. Use it when approvals, reporting, and account management should not be split across unrelated logins. The pricing page listed three per-seat monthly figures for this comparison: Standard at $199, Professional at $299, and Advanced at $399. Those are per seat, so a team of three Standard seats at the listed rate is 199 × 3 = 597 dollars per month. Three Professional seats are 299 × 3 = 897. Three Advanced seats are 399 × 3 = 1,197. That arithmetic is illustrative. It assumes the listed per-seat rate applies to every seat and that no other discount is on the quote. It is not a second price Sprout published for a three-seat pack. Reopen [the pricing page](https://sproutsocial.com/pricing/) and read the billing period before you send the number to finance.

### Meltwater

Meltwater is built for media intelligence, social listening, AI visibility tracking, media relations, and influencer marketing. Meltwater states that its platform ingests 1.3 billion or more documents a day and supports 240 or more languages. Both figures are Meltwater's platform claims, published on [Meltwater's site](https://www.meltwater.com/en). They describe intake and language coverage as Meltwater states them. They do not say how many of those documents mention your brand, and they do not say that a mention was classified correctly. Pricing is custom. A trial or a sales conversation is where you test whether the sources you care about are actually in the index.

### Brandwatch

Brandwatch fits companies that treat social data as market research rather than as a scheduling problem. Use it when consumer intelligence and audience research matter more than the content calendar. Data coverage, price, and the sentiment method are vendor-specific. Verify them in a trial. This page does not quote a Brandwatch price or an accuracy rate.

## What fits a smaller team without an enterprise contract?

Smaller teams usually need one loop done well: alerts, an inbox, or a short-form report. Buying the widest index is how the tool goes unused.

### Brand24

Brand24 is a focused monitor for brand mentions and reputation. Brand24 states that it tracks 25 million online sources in real time, and that coverage includes social media, news, blogs, videos, forums, podcasts, reviews, and more. That is Brand24's claim, on [Brand24's site](https://brand24.com/). Sentiment in a tool like this is worth reading and worth checking by hand. This page does not claim a universal accuracy percentage, because the source for this comparison does not support one.

### Agorapulse

Agorapulse is a fit for agencies that publish, assign, reply, and report from connected social accounts. The core object is the inbox and the calendar, not a crawl of the open web. Do not write the requirement "tell me every forum mention by morning" into an Agorapulse trial and then call the product incomplete. That requirement is a listening requirement.

### Metricool

Metricool combines planning, analytics, reporting, and competitor tracking for smaller teams. On the US pricing table checked on 13 September 2026, Metricool listed a free plan for one brand and Starter from $25 per month. "From $25" means the listed starting figure, not a guarantee that every configuration is $25. The source for that check is [Metricool's pricing page](https://metricool.com/pricing/). Competitor profile analysis inside a planner is still not a full mention index. You can see how a tracked profile's public posts behave and still miss a Reddit thread that never tags the profile.

### Buffer

Buffer is the minimalist choice for scheduling and a basic review of channels you connect. Checked on 13 September 2026, Buffer listed a free plan for up to three channels, and Essentials at $5 per channel per month on yearly billing. The source is [Buffer's pricing page](https://buffer.com/pricing). A team that needs a fourth channel does not fit the free-plan limit of three. Four channels at the listed Essentials per-channel rate would be 4 × 5 = 20 dollars per month on yearly billing. That product is illustrative. It assumes the per-channel rate stays $5 when you add channels, which you must confirm on the page, and it says nothing about monthly billing.

An illustrative gap between starting prices is easy to misuse. Metricool Starter from $25 and one Sprout Social Standard seat at $199 differ by 199 − 25 = 174 dollars at those listed monthly starting figures. The $174 is not savings. The two products do not do the same job. Subtracting them answers "what is the arithmetic difference between two listed starting prices?" It does not answer "which one finds more mentions?"

## How should you read a vendor coverage number?

A coverage number is a claim about the vendor's index. Read it with four checks before it enters a deck.

1. Name the speaker. "Meltwater states 1.3 billion or more documents a day" is a different sentence from "the internet produces 1.3 billion brand mentions a day."
2. Name the date you saw it. Buffer and Metricool figures on this page were checked on 13 September 2026. Sprout Social's per-seat figures are what the pricing page listed for this comparison. None of them are self-updating.
3. Ask what the number counts. Documents ingested are not mentions of you. Online sources are not posts about you. Languages supported are not languages in which your name was found this week.
4. Refuse a borrowed accuracy rate. If the vendor does not publish a method you can restate, do not invent a percentage for the slide.

Sentiment is the field where this fails most often. A useful vendor explains where the classifier works and where a person still has to read the post. A demo that offers only a green-and-red donut has not explained the method.

## What should a monitoring trial actually test?

The trial should test the job you wrote down, on sources you already know about. A canned brand query that returns flattering posts is not a test.

Work through this list and keep the vendor's answer next to each line:

- Real-time alerts: does the tool notify you within five minutes of a mention? Five minutes is the question to ask. It is not a standard this page claims every product meets.
- Sentiment: does the vendor say where a person still needs to review the label?
- Source breadth: does the index include the places you actually care about, such as Reddit, TikTok, news, forums, podcasts, or reviews?
- Competitive context: can you see your trail next to a named rival, and is that view public data, a connected account, or a model?
- Inbox boundary: are replies limited to accounts you connect?
- Export: can you leave with the rows your weekly review uses?

An illustrative one-week trial keeps the jobs from collapsing into each other. Pick one brand name you can check by hand. On day one, count mentions you already found without the tool. On days two and three, see whether the listening tool finds that set, plus anything you missed. On days four and five, answer the same posts from the inbox tool, and note which posts the inbox cannot see because the account is not connected. On days six and seven, pull a public short-form sample and tag hooks, without asking the listening tool to invent a watch-time figure. Seven days, three jobs, three notes. If one note is empty, that product failed the job on the note, not "monitoring" in the abstract.
