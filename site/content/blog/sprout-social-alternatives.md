The best Sprout Social alternative depends on the job you are replacing, not on one ranked product. Sprout Social is a broad social media management suite. Its [pricing page](https://sproutsocial.com/pricing/) lists Standard at $199 per seat per month, Professional at $299, and Advanced at $399, plus Essentials at $79 per seat per month with monthly billing shown at $99. Pay that when one workspace must cover publishing, a shared inbox, listening, reporting, and governance. Do not pay it for a scheduler, a competitor report, or public short-form research alone. Metricool, Buffer, Agorapulse, and Socialinsider cover operations and reporting at different scopes. ContHunt searches public TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. ContHunt is not a Sprout Social replacement and does not provide approvals, an inbox, CRM integrations, or a listening suite.

## What job should a Sprout Social alternative actually cover?

A Sprout Social alternative is a tool that takes one job Sprout Social is paid for. It is not a cheaper clone of the whole suite. Teams blend three jobs into one vendor search, then reject a fit because it fails a job it was never built to do.

Publishing and inbox work means composing, approving, scheduling, and answering people in a shared queue. Competitor reporting means benchmarking accounts you do not own and exporting that comparison on a schedule. Creative research means studying public short-form videos, hooks, formats, and captions before anyone writes a brief. Sprout Social can cover the first job and much of the second, with listening and governance as further modules. Public creative research on TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts is a different dataset from your own account analytics.

| Job | What done looks like | Closer alternatives | Poor comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Publishing and inbox | Posts leave on time, replies have an owner, approvals are visible | Agorapulse, Metricool, Buffer | A research tool with no calendar |
| Competitor reporting | A repeatable export of public account performance | Socialinsider, Metricool | A scheduler that cannot add competitor profiles |
| Creative research | A ranked set of public videos that answer one brief | ContHunt | Any suite that only shows accounts you manage |

Use Sprout Social when one governed workspace for publishing, engagement, analytics, listening, and enterprise workflow is worth the per-seat price. Use a modular stack when scheduling, reporting, and research can live in separate tools without hurting collaboration. Use ContHunt only in the third row. ContHunt supports public short-form research and creative briefing. It is not a private account analytics dashboard and it is not a social inbox.

## What does Sprout Social list at each price?

Sprout Social prices by seat, not by channel. On the [pricing page](https://sproutsocial.com/pricing/), Standard is $199 per seat per month, Professional is $299 per seat per month, and Advanced is $399 per seat per month. Enterprise is custom. Those three rates are the same figures this comparison recorded on September 13, 2026. The page now also lists Essentials at $79 per seat per month, with $99 per seat per month billed monthly beside it. The trial header refers to annual billing. Confirm on your own visit whether the Standard, Professional, and Advanced figures are the annual rate before you line them up against another vendor's monthly invoice.

The plan comparison on that page lists social profile caps of 5 on Essentials, 5 on Standard, and unlimited on Professional and Advanced. Brand keywords are not included on Essentials. They are listed as 10 on Standard, 10 on Professional, and 20 on Advanced. Every plan includes Trellis, Sprout's AI agent, at 100 credits per seat each month. Trellis Plus is a separate add-on at 1,000 credits per seat each month, listed at $28 per seat per month, or $35 per seat per month billed monthly. Credits are a capacity limit on that assistant. They are not a reason to pick a research tool or a scheduler.

An illustrative seat check uses only those listed rates. Three people on Standard is 3 × $199 = $597 per month before add-ons. Two people on Professional is 2 × $299 = $598 per month. One person on Essentials is $79 per month at the $79 figure, or $99 per month at the monthly-billing figure. A small team on Sprout Social is often a seat-count problem. If four people need a login and only one of them publishes, the quote still moves with seats.

Listening and Premium Analytics are add-ons from Standard upward. The pricing page does not print a public dollar amount for those add-ons. Employee advocacy and professional services are also add-ons from Standard upward. Influencer Marketing and NewsWhip are presented as separate products that do not require a Sprout Social plan. Do not assume Standard includes a full listening deployment. Read the add-on block before you call the suite expensive or cheap next to a tool that never sold listening.

Sprout Social stays rational when the team uses the governed inbox, the higher-plan helpdesk or API access, message controls, and stakeholder reporting in one login. It is a weak default when the invoice mostly pays for seats that open the product to approve a caption.

## When is Socialinsider the closer reporting alternative?

Socialinsider is a social analytics and competitor-analysis product. It is not a full publishing suite. Its [pricing page](https://www.socialinsider.io/pricing) says every plan includes cross-network analytics, competitor benchmarking, automated reports, unlimited exports, an MCP connector, and priority support. That is the slice many agencies think they are buying inside Sprout Social, then barely use the inbox beside it.

The page lists Adapt, Optimize, Predict, and a custom Enterprise plan. Adapt is shown at $82, with yearly billing displayed as $990 per year next to a struck-through $1,188. Optimize is shown at $124. Predict is shown at $199. The page has a monthly and yearly toggle and describes yearly plans as two months off, so read the toggle before you paste a number into a budget. A 14-day trial is offered and the page says no credit card is required.

Account and history limits on that page differ by plan:

- Adapt covers up to 20 social accounts, 3 months of historical data, 1 seat, and 10 profile replacements per month.
- Optimize covers up to 30 social accounts, 6 months of history, 2 seats, and 15 replacements per month.
- Predict covers up to 40 social accounts, 12 months of history, 5 seats, and 20 replacements per month.
- Enterprise is custom on profiles, history, seats, and replacements.

Add-ons listed for the packaged plans are additional profiles at $6 per month per profile, additional team members at $15 per month, a reset of monthly replacements at $2 per profile, API access at $100 per month, and Looker Studio access at $100 per month.

Illustrative add-on math, not a quote: Adapt's displayed yearly figure of $990 plus two extra seats at $15 each is $990 + (2 × $15 × 12) = $990 + $360 = $1,350 for the year, before extra profiles. Five extra profiles at $6 is 5 × $6 = $30 per month. Socialinsider can cost less than a multi-seat Sprout Social plan and still not schedule the client's posts or run a support inbox. If the agency sells both a report and day-to-day publishing, pair Socialinsider with a scheduler. Do not treat the analytics login as the management suite.

Socialinsider is the closer Sprout Social alternative for benchmarking and exports. It does not replace approvals, social customer care, or a listening program Sprout sells as an add-on.

## When is Agorapulse the closer inbox alternative?

Agorapulse is the nearer fit when the Sprout Social workflow you cannot drop is publishing, engagement, a unified inbox, and team reporting. The vendor prices Standard and higher plans by user count and profile count. This article does not quote an Agorapulse dollar figure. A current public price was not part of the sourced numbers here, so read it on Agorapulse's pricing page before you decide.

Choose Agorapulse for support-heavy teams that live in replies. Comments, messages, and assignments are the point of the product. Do not choose it as a database of short-form creative patterns. A unified inbox shows what people said to accounts you manage. It does not show which public hooks are appearing for other brands on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts.

If the must-have list is a shared inbox, approvals, and a client-ready report, Agorapulse belongs on the shortlist next to Sprout Social. If the must-have list is what the next set of videos should learn from, an inbox product does not answer it. Split the purchase instead of forcing one login to do both.

## How does Metricool compare for a small business?

Metricool is the small-business comparison when you want planning, publishing, analytics, reporting, and some competitor profiles without a per-seat enterprise quote. The [pricing page](https://metricool.com/pricing/) shows euros and dollars, and it shows annual and monthly billing. Every dollar figure below is from the US dollar tables. Do not mix those cells with the euro columns, and do not mix the monthly column with the annual column.

The free plan is $0 per month. It includes 1 brand, scheduling of up to 20 posts per month, 5 competitor profiles, and 30 days of analytics. The page says the free plan covers the brand's social networks except LinkedIn and Twitter/X.

On the USD monthly table, Starter starts at $25 per month. That table lists up to 5 brands for $25 per month and up to 10 brands for $45 per month. On the USD annual table, Starter starts at $20 per month, with up to 5 brands at $20 per month and up to 10 brands at $36 per month. Starter cards list analysis of up to 100 competitors, analytics history beyond the free plan's 30 days, PDF and PPT reports, and a LinkedIn connection. The page says unlimited publishing on paid plans can still be limited by Metricool's fair use policy, so "unlimited" is not a promise of infinite volume.

On the USD monthly table, Advanced starts at $67 per month for up to 15 brands, $107 per month for up to 25 brands, and $210 per month for up to 50 brands. On the USD annual table, Advanced starts at $53 per month for up to 15 brands, $85 per month for up to 25 brands, and $159 per month for up to 50 brands. Advanced is where that page places team and client management, roles, a post approval system, and a Data Studio connector.

One illustrative contrast, using listed rates and not a claim that the products match: Metricool Starter at the USD monthly price for 5 brands is $25 per month, which is 12 × $25 = $300 over a year. One Sprout Social Standard seat at $199 is 12 × $199 = $2,388 over a year before add-ons. A second Sprout seat doubles that Sprout line. Metricool at $25 does not include Sprout's listening add-on or the same governance model. The gap is only meaningful after you have crossed off the Sprout modules nobody will open.

Metricool is a strong Sprout Social alternative for small teams and for agencies that want several brands in one planner. A fuller walk-through of that price ladder is in [Metricool pricing and alternatives](/blog/metricool-pricing-alternatives). Metricool is still a management and analytics workspace. It does not replace a pass through public short-form video before a creative brief.

## How does Buffer price a simpler publishing stack?

Buffer is the per-channel option for straightforward scheduling and light analytics. Its [pricing page](https://buffer.com/pricing) lists a free plan for up to 3 channels, with 10 scheduled posts per channel, 1 user, and insights limited to a 30-day history. Essentials is $5 per month for one channel on yearly billing, shown as $60 billed yearly, with 1 user and unlimited scheduled posts on that plan. Team is $10 per month for one channel on yearly billing, shown as $120 billed yearly, with unlimited team members, access levels, and content approval workflows. The page shows a 14-day trial path on the paid plans.

Per-channel pricing is kind to a brand with two networks and less kind to a brand that posts everywhere. Illustrative yearly-billing math: 4 channels on Essentials is 4 × $5 = $20 per month, and 12 × $20 = $240 for the year. Four channels on Team is 4 × $10 = $40 per month, and 12 × $40 = $480 for the year. Eight channels on Team is 8 × $10 = $80 per month. Set that next to the three-seat Standard illustration of $597 per month only as a scope check. Buffer Team's approvals and community tools are a lighter control set than Sprout's customer-care reports, helpdesk integrations, and listening add-ons. If you need sentiment in a smart inbox or listening spike alerts, Buffer is the wrong alternative no matter how attractive the channel math looks.

Buffer is the Sprout Social alternative for solo operators and small teams that want predictable publishing. It is a weak alternative for an agency whose deliverable is competitor benchmarking. Pair it with a reporting product if the client report is what you sell.

## How do you choose without running a feature bake-off?

Pick a Sprout Social alternative with a short purchasing procedure. A logo grid will not tell you which invoice matches the work.

1. Write the jobs from the last 30 days: publish, approve, reply, report, listen, research. A job that did not happen is not a requirement.
2. Mark which jobs need a shared login. Seat price matters for those people, not for a logo on a slide.
3. Count networks and brands. Buffer's price moves with channels. Metricool's price moves with brands. Sprout Social's price moves with seats. Socialinsider's packaged plans move with account caps, history, and seats.
4. Split "accounts we manage" from "public videos we do not own." The first is analytics and publishing. The second is research.
5. Price one month from the vendor's current page, including add-ons you will turn on. Listening, Trellis Plus, extra Socialinsider seats, and extra profiles change the total. A base plan screenshot usually understates it.
6. Pilot the finalist on one real week: one approval chain, one inbox day, one client export, or one research question. A trial that never touches the job you are replacing does not test the alternative.

| Team shape | Start the shortlist here | Leave off the shortlist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One person, a few channels, scheduling only | Buffer free or Essentials | Sprout Advanced and a listening add-on |
| Small brand that wants a planner, analytics, and a few competitors | Metricool free, then Starter if the free caps bind | A per-seat suite priced for cross-functional governance |
| Agency that delivers competitor reports | Socialinsider, plus Metricool if you also publish | A research tool presented as the whole reporting suite |
| Support team with a shared inbox | Agorapulse or Sprout Social | A scheduler with no assignment model |
| Creative team briefing short-form video | ContHunt beside whichever publisher you keep | Any publisher sold as a hook database |

## What breaks if you treat the products as the same thing?

History does not move because someone exported a PDF. Socialinsider caps history at 3 months on Adapt, 6 months on Optimize, and 12 months on Predict. Metricool's free plan stores 30 days of analytics. Buffer's free insights cover 30 days, while Essentials and Team list unlimited history. A team that has lived in Sprout Social for years often depends on tagged inbox history and a reporting habit, not on the composer. Budget a parallel month if stakeholders still expect the old report shape.

Governance is the other quiet loss. Approvals on Buffer Team or on Metricool Advanced are not the same control set as an enterprise social program with helpdesk integrations and customer-care reports on higher Sprout plans. If legal reviews every public reply, write that down before a lower seat price looks like a win.

Creative research is the gap teams notice later. A cheaper scheduler still will not show which public opening is holding attention in the category. If that question is on the weekly agenda, give it a tool that watches public video. Do not cancel a listening contract because a research workflow exists, and do not skip the videos because a listening contract exists. They answer different questions.
