ContHunt is the short-form research tool for deciding when a public pile is enough to brief. It watches the TikToks. You stop when the job sentence stops changing. A round number of videos is not that decision.

The page that writes the three openings comes after this one. [How to research TikTok hooks before you brief a creator](/resources/research-tiktok-hooks-before-you-brief-a-creator) is that page. This page only answers how many public videos to score before you are allowed to start it.

The stop is two scored videos that share one job sentence, plus the next ten scored videos leaving that sentence where it is. ContHunt can watch a large public pile. The brief does not take the pile. A scroll does not count, and a video you saved without writing the score down does not count. Each video in the ten is scored on the opening, the proof, the words, the comments, and a public ratio only when the post shows one. A video about a different job never enters the ten. If one of the ten needs a new sentence, or the proof moves off the first frame into a later greeting, throw the check out and score a fresh ten. A fixed watch count fails when the videos are the same greeting repeated. You can stop before the niche runs out. You cannot stop because one video had a lot of plays.

## What are you counting?

Count videos you scored. A scroll does not count. A video you saved and did not write down does not count. [How to score a TikTok you do not own](/resources/how-to-score-a-tiktok-you-do-not-own) is the rubric: the opening, the proof, whether the words match the footage, the comment job, and the public ratio when the post shows one. Retention stays blank. The account that posted the video has it. You do not.

Write the job sentence before the first video. The hook page gives the shape: who the video is for, what they should believe or do, and the proof they will see. A sentence you write after the tenth viral video is a description of what you happened to watch. It will not filter the next one.

Keep two notes beside the count.

| Note | What you write | What forces a new check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Job sentence | One line, written first | A scored video that needs a different line |
| Proof | Where it appears, in seconds | A scored video that still fits the sentence, with the proof in a different place |

Likes, plays, and follower count are not on the note. A higher number does not mean you have watched enough.

## When do you stop?

Stop when both of these are true.

1. Two scored videos share the job sentence. [How to compare two TikToks without analytics](/resources/how-to-compare-two-tiktoks-without-analytics) is the sheet for that pair. The winner is the video whose proof a viewer can see sooner, and whose comments ask something your product can answer.
2. The next ten scored videos do not change the sentence, and do not move the proof.

Ten is the check, not a quota you collect. Each of the ten is scored against the same sentence. A video about a different job never enters the ten. It was filtered out before the stop.

If one of the ten needs a new sentence, or the proof moves from the first frame to a later greeting, throw the check out. Rewrite the sentence. Score a fresh ten. Do not add the failed ten to a running total and call that progress.

You can stop before the niche runs out of videos. You cannot stop because one video had a lot of plays.

## Why does a fixed number fail?

"Watch 10" fails when all ten open with a greeting. "Watch 100" fails when all hundred are the same stitch. "Watch until you are tired" fails because tired is a fact about you, not about the niche.

[Deep Search](/resources/how-deep-search-works) can watch 500 to 1,000 public videos on TikTok, Reels, and Shorts and score them against the question you asked. That is the size of a pile a person should not scroll by hand. It is not the number of videos that belong in a brief. The same page says to report the real match count when the niche is thin. If 70 videos fit, say 70. If fewer than about 50 fit, widen the window instead of trusting a pattern. Your stop can arrive inside that pile. Reaching the end of the pile is not the brief.

TikTok's [creative best practices](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/creative-best-practices) tell advertisers to introduce the point in the opening seconds. That is why the proof's place belongs in the stop. A sentence that stays the same while the proof slides from the first frame to second twelve has not held. The person you brief will only meet that later proof if the viewer stays, and you cannot see whether they stayed.

## Do you score every video the tool watched?

No. The tool's watched set is the input. The stop runs on the videos you are willing to cite.

Open the videos the answer attaches. A pattern you cannot see in those videos is not ready to brief, which is the same rule Deep Search already states for its own result. Draw the pair, and then the ten, from that cited set. Do not start a second scroll to make the count feel larger. Do not hand-score the full 500. The point of the watch is that you do not have to.

If the cited set has no two videos that share a sentence, you do not have a pattern yet. Widen the window, or write the brief as a guess and say so. One strong video plus a pile of near misses is still one video.

## What does a worked stop look like?

The counts below are an illustration of the note. They are not a pull from a creator's analytics.

The product is a serum for reactive skin. The sentence, written first: people with reactive skin should believe it will not sting, because they see a patch test.

The first eight scored videos are morning routines. The bottle shows up after a greeting. They name the product and miss the proof, so they do not enter the ten. They also do not change the sentence. They fail the filter, and they leave the sentence where it was.

Video nine opens on a forearm with two squares of product and says the patch test out loud. Video ten is a duet that keeps that frame and asks whether reactive skin can use it. Those two share the sentence. On the comparison sheet, the patch-test video wins. The proof is in the first frame, and the comments ask the next question.

The next ten scored videos are more routines, one joke that never shows skin, and one more patch test. The joke is out, because the product is not the joke. The routines stay out. The extra patch test does not move the proof and does not change the sentence. The check holds. Stop.

Nineteen videos were scored in this illustration. The rest of a 500-video watch can stay in the pile. Those videos are not extra briefs. The brief gets the winning opening, the winning proof, and the sentence that survived the ten.

If your product cannot show a patch test, the winner is still not your script. It is the standard. Film a proof you actually have, in the first shot, and keep the sentence you wrote before you pressed play.

## What if the ten never holds?

Three failed checks mean the sentence is doing two jobs. Split it. "Show that it will not sting" and "show what it costs" are two stops. Running them as one sentence guarantees the proof will keep moving, because the videos are answering different questions.

A check that only holds on jokes, with no product in the frame, is a check about entertainment. Leave it out of a product brief. A funny comment thread can be the product only when the joke is the thing you sell. A serum is not that case.

If a third rewrite still moves on every ten, stop collecting and change the search. The words in the sentence may not be the words creators use. The hook page is where that vocabulary gets built. Come back to this stop after the sentence uses those words. Do not lower the check to five so that a noisy niche looks finished.

## What if one account is the whole pile?

One account is a week of that creator, not a sample of the job. [What to measure daily](/resources/competitor-content-tracking-what-to-measure-daily) is the page for following accounts across days. Use it when the question is whether this creator kept the same proof. Do not use a single profile as the ten. Seven greetings in a row would pass the check and tell you nothing about anyone else.

The pile for this stop is every public video that can meet the sentence, including accounts you will never hire. Hiring is a question about an account. The brief is a question about a video.

## Does the same stop work on a Reel or a Short?

The stop is the same rule. The pair is not interchangeable. Compare two videos from the platform you will publish on. A TikTok that wins on a stitch is not evidence that a Reel caption should repeat it. If the sentence holds on one platform and breaks on another, write that in the note. Do not average TikTok, Reels, and Shorts into one opening.

Score a Reel or a Short with the same five checks. Private insights stay on the account that posted it. A public like count still does not reset the ten, on any of the three.

## What do you write down when you stop?

Four lines. Then leave this page and write the brief.

1. The job sentence that survived the ten.
2. The two scores, and which video won the sheet.
3. The winner's first line, and where the proof sits, in seconds.
4. Keep, test, or drop. Keep means the brief may use that opening. Test means you will film your own proof in that place. Drop means the other video.

The ten stay as evidence that the sentence held. They do not each become a hook. Three openings, written from the winner, are the next page.
