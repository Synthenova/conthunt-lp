A YouTube Short is a short-form video YouTube can show in the Shorts feed, and in 2026 it can be up to 3 minutes long. YouTube's help page on creating Shorts says the Shorts camera creates Shorts up to three minutes, and that recorded clips add up to three minutes. That ceiling is not a brief to fill. Use about 15 to 35 seconds for a fast hook, a reaction, or a single tip. Use about 35 to 60 seconds for a tutorial with real steps. Use 60 to 180 seconds only for a story, a test, or a ranked breakdown where each beat earns the next. If the idea needs more than 3 minutes, publish a standard video and let a Short carry one promise from it.

## What is the maximum length of a YouTube Short?

The maximum length of a YouTube Short is 3 minutes, provided the upload qualifies as a Short. [YouTube's create-Shorts instructions](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10343433) describe recording in the Shorts camera and cutting together clips that add up to three minutes.

Three minutes is 180 seconds. A file at 181 seconds is past the Shorts ceiling this guide plans around. Publish that idea as a standard video.

The ceiling comes with a few neighboring rules on the same help page, and they change how you plan a long Short even though they are not "length" in the narrow sense.

- You can upload Short videos with a maximum resolution of 1080p. A longer timeline does not buy you a higher upload resolution.
- The title on the details screen has a maximum of 100 characters.
- Depending on the song, you may be able to add up to 90 seconds of music in a Short of up to 3 minutes when you use YouTube's tools. A 3 minute picture does not mean 3 minutes of a given track.
- Shorts longer than one minute with an active copyright claim will no longer be automatically blocked and may remain playable. A claim can still change what the Short is allowed to earn. Length does not make a claim irrelevant.
- Where Edit with AI is offered, the help page says you can upload up to 3 minutes of assets in total, with a minimum of 5 seconds, and that the feature is not available in every region. The raw-material cap matches the Short cap. It does not ask you to use all of it.

Check the help page when you are unsure, because a creator remembering "Shorts are 60 seconds" is remembering an older ceiling. The practical checklist under the current one is short. Keep the frame vertical or square enough to qualify as a Short. Stay at or under 3 minutes. Make the opening frame and the title clear enough that a stranger can tell what the Short is. Do not add shots just because the limit is longer than the idea.

## Which length should you start from?

Start from the shortest band that can finish the job, and treat the table below as a starting point rather than a rule YouTube publishes. The bands overlap on purpose.

A joke and a tip are not the same shape, even when both are "short."

| Goal | Starting length | Why this band is the first cut |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fast tip | 15–25 seconds | One idea, one payoff, few places to leave |
| Entertainment hook | 20–35 seconds | Enough time for a setup and a punchline |
| Tutorial | 35–60 seconds | Room for steps without a second intro |
| Product demo | 45–90 seconds | Time for the proof, the feature, and the result |
| Story or breakdown | 60–180 seconds | Works only when each beat keeps tension |

Read the fast-hook rows together. Simple ideas, entertainment, reactions, and direct tips sit in a combined band of about 15 to 35 seconds. Many Shorts that hold attention land between 15 and 60 seconds, which covers the tip, the entertainment hook, and the tutorial row. The rows past 60 seconds are not a promotion you unlock by posting more. They are a different format. A ranked list can use them. A single tip almost never can.

The right duration inside a band is the version with the fewest slow moments, not the version that touches the top of the band. A 25 second tip that repeats the tip in new words is worse than a 17 second tip that shows it once. A 90 second demo that shows the result in the first seconds and then proves how is a different object from a 90 second demo that hides the result until the end.

## When should a YouTube Short stay under 30 seconds?

A YouTube Short should stay under 30 seconds when the idea is one thing. One thing means the viewer can retell it in a single sentence after one watch.

If the retelling needs a list, you are no longer in this bucket, and forcing the list into 25 seconds will blur every item.

Keep the Short in that bucket for:

- one visual transformation
- one mistake to avoid
- one tool recommendation
- one joke or reaction
- one before-and-after result

These are easier to rewatch, easier to test, and easier to compare in your analytics, because the whole video is the point. There is no "middle" you have to excuse. If you are still learning what this audience stays for, start here and lengthen only after retention says people wanted the rest. Retention here means what you see on your own video in YouTube, not a score guessed from the outside.

A short Short still needs a real opening frame. The first picture should be the transformation, the mistake, the tool, the reaction, or the after. A title card that says the topic, held for two seconds, is a tax on a format that only had 20 seconds to begin with. Speak the topic while the picture is already the evidence.

## When do longer YouTube Shorts make sense?

Longer YouTube Shorts make sense when the viewer wants more than a quick payoff and you can keep a new beat arriving. The extra time has to be spent on progression.

A middle that repeats the opening claim in softer words is the same Short with a longer timeline, and the timeline is what people leave.

Formats that can earn 60 to 180 seconds:

- a "tested this for 7 days" story, with a result that is not visible in one frame
- a ranked list with a clear progression, so item three is not a restatement of item one
- a mini tutorial whose steps fail if you skip one
- a creator breakdown that shows the original choice and then the reason
- a product or app walkthrough with a feature and a result
- a case study that withholds the ending until the viewer has the facts

The test is tension, not topic importance. A serious subject can still be a 20 second Short if the contribution is one fact. A light subject can earn two minutes if the viewer is waiting for a specific outcome, such as whether the test failed. "I need two minutes because the topic is valuable" is not a structure. "The viewer cannot know the outcome until the last step, and each step changes what they know" is a structure.

Cut the moment a beat repeats. If the middle section makes the same point as the open, the middle section goes, even if the music swell is nice. The edit is finished when nothing is left to remove, not when the file approaches 3 minutes.

Here is an illustrative cut, not a measured upload. A product-demo draft runs 120 seconds. One feature is shown twice, and the repeat is 25 seconds. A 15 second shot of the unopened box never shows the result. Removing the repeat and the box leaves 120 − 25 − 15 = 80 seconds. Eighty seconds sits in the 45 to 90 second demo band. The draft was not a two-minute idea. It was an 80 second idea with a duplicate and a prologue.

## How do you decide the length before you upload?

Decide a YouTube Short's length in the edit, before you upload, by tying every span to the promise and marking the first place you get bored. Upload is a bad moment to discover that the middle sags.

You will talk yourself into "it's only two minutes." The viewer will not.

1. Write the promise in one sentence. This is the sentence a stranger should be able to repeat.
2. Cut every clip that does not support that promise, including clips you like.
3. Watch the draft without audio. Music hides a static frame. Silence does not.
4. Mark the first moment your attention slows. That moment is the cut, or the place you replace the picture.
5. Shorten or rebuild that section before you upload. Do not "fix it in the title."

Then compare the draft's length with the job. A fast tip that survived the five steps and still runs 70 seconds is probably still hiding a second intro. A breakdown that survived and runs 40 seconds may be finished, and stretching it to a minute would put the slow moment back. Stop when the promise is paid. The 3 minute ceiling is permission, not a quota.

Public Shorts in the same topic are a useful argument, if you treat them as structure. ContHunt searches TikTok, Instagram Reels, and YouTube Shorts. Deep Search watches 500 to 1,000 videos in under five minutes and returns a ranked answer with the videos attached as evidence. Ask which lengths the Shorts in this cluster actually use, and what they show before the first spoken claim. That answer is a judgment of the videos, not a read of anyone's private retention. If the Shorts worth studying are 25 second demonstrations, a 2 minute explanation of the same move is likely the wrong format, even though 2 minutes is legal. Confirm the choice with your own retention after you post. Analytics for that read are covered in [YouTube Shorts analytics](/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics).

## How do music, claims, and packaging limit a long Short?

Music, copyright claims, and packaging limit a long YouTube Short even when the picture is under 3 minutes. The timeline is not the only clock.

The music clock is easy to miss. YouTube says that, depending on the song, you may be able to add up to 90 seconds of music in Shorts of up to 3 minutes when you use YouTube's tools. Plan the track as its own asset. If the Short is 150 seconds and the track you want covers 90, the last minute of picture needs another audio plan: original sound, a second permitted segment, or silence under the voice. Do not assume the library will score the whole file. Selecting an audio in YouTube's tools shows how many seconds that music will cover. Read that number before you cut the picture to it.

The claim clock is the one-minute mark YouTube calls out. Shorts longer than one minute with an active copyright claim are no longer automatically blocked, and they may remain playable. "May remain playable" is not "cleared," and it is not advice to use someone else's song. If you want to avoid the claim, use music from the Shorts audio library, which is the path the help page points to. A Short you hope to monetize should not discover its audio problem after it is public. The upload flow is there so you can see the claim first.

Packaging has its own limits, and length does not relax them. The title field maxes at 100 characters on the details screen. A 3 minute Short does not get a longer title. Spend the characters on the promise, not on a sentence that only makes sense after someone has watched. The opening frame has to carry the same promise, because plenty of viewers meet the Short as a picture before they hear the audio.

Thumbnails are a separate choice from duration. YouTube's update on [making thumbnails easier](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-studio-custom-thumbnail-updates/) says YouTube Partner Program creators can upload custom thumbnails for Shorts, with that option expanding to more creators over time. On desktop, you can choose from three suggested frames for a Short. In the YouTube app you can still pick any frame from the video. The same post describes thumbnail generation inside Ask Studio for long-form videos, which is a different workflow from a Short. How to choose the frame itself is in [YouTube Shorts thumbnail best practices](/blog/youtube-shorts-thumbnail-best-practices-2026). A long Short with a vague frame wastes the extra time you fought to keep, because the click or the swipe never reaches the second minute.

The help page also notes a Get feedback control on the way to upload, for a look at hook and pacing. Use it as a prompt to rewatch the open. It is not a measurement of how the Short will perform, and it does not replace the retention curve after the Short is live.

## What if the idea is honestly longer than 3 minutes?

If the idea is honestly longer than 3 minutes, it is not a Short. Publish the full piece as a standard YouTube video, and cut a Short that pays off one promise from inside it.

The Short can be the discovery hook. It should not be the full piece played at a higher speed, and it should not be a trailer that withholds everything. A Short that only says "full video on my channel" without a complete moment of its own gives the viewer no reason to believe the long video is better.

Pick the moment that stands alone. A single result, a single step people get wrong, or a single turn in the story can be a 20 to 40 second Short. The long video keeps the setup, the exceptions, and the second example. Link them with YouTube's related-video tools if you use them, so the person who wants the rest has a path. Do not slow the Short down to gesture at chapters you did not include. Gesture is how a 40 second idea becomes a 150 second shrug.

The edit of that Short follows the same five steps as any other Short. The existence of a long video is not a reason to leave the repeats in.

## How should you review length after the Short is up?

Review length after the Short is up by looking at where people leave, relative to the promise, and by refusing to repeat a runtime that your own viewers do not finish. The curve on the video is the check the planning table cannot do.

A 25 second demonstration that people finish is a better template for the next tip than a 2 minute explanation of a similar tip that loses them in the middle. The 2 minute cut might still be the right shape for a different promise. It is not the right shape for this one, and "but 2 minutes is allowed" does not change the curve.

Compare like with like. A comedy Short and a tutorial will not teach you a universal best duration. Group by the rows in the table: tips with tips, demos with demos, stories with stories. If inside one group the shorter cuts hold and the longer cuts sag in the same place, the sag is the edit. Fix the next one before you blame the topic.

When you look at other channels for a length cue, keep the cue in its place. A public pattern is a reason to test. Your retention is the reason to keep the test. The editing pass that removes the slow middle is covered in [How to edit YouTube Shorts](/blog/how-to-edit-youtube-shorts).

## YouTube Shorts length checklist

Run this YouTube Shorts length checklist before the video goes public. It catches a file that is allowed as an upload and still wrong as a Short.

- The Short is at or under 3 minutes, and the frame is vertical or square enough to qualify.
- The first second shows the promise, not a title card that delays it.
- The length matches the job: about 15 to 35 seconds for a fast hook, 35 to 60 for a tutorial, 60 to 180 only for a story or breakdown that keeps tension.
- Every section moves the viewer toward the payoff. A repeated beat is gone.
- The same idea was considered at half the length, and the longer version survived that cut.
- Music length was checked against YouTube's limit for that track, including the 90 second cap when it applies.
- A copyright claim was reviewed before the Short went public, especially if the file is over one minute.
- The title is within the 100 character field, and the opening frame agrees with it.
- Retention will be read on this video before the format is repeated.
