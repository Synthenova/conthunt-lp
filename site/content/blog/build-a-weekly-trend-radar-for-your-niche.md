A weekly trend radar is a 45-minute routine that sorts formats, sounds, topics and products in one niche into emerging, rising, peaking and fading, then writes a decision beside every row. Trends are cheap to spot and expensive to catch late. By the time a format shows up in your feed three times, other brands have already shot their version. The radar tells you which patterns are early enough to act on and which have passed.

It is a routine, not a dashboard. The output is one page, reviewed once a week, with a decision beside every row.

## What are the four lenses?

Four lenses keep the radar readable: formats, sounds, topics and claims, and products, tracked separately because they do not move on the same clock. Mixing them on one undifferentiated list blurs which kind of pattern you would actually shoot.

| Lens | What you track | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Formats | Repeatable video structures | "Three things I stopped buying" |
| Sounds | Audio that creators in your niche reuse | A voiceover meme, a song snippet |
| Topics and claims | What people are talking about | An ingredient, a routine, a debate |
| Products | Items showing up across creators | A gadget, a flavor, a bundle |

Formats and sounds tend to move fast. Topics and products often last longer. That is a working assumption to test on your own niche, since every category differs.

## What are the four states?

Four states label every item each week: emerging, rising, peaking and fading, judged from creator count, recent videos against each creator's norm, and how crowded the pattern has become. Adjust the thresholds to the size of your niche. These definitions are starting rules.

| State | Signs | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Emerging | Fewer than 5 creators, but recent videos run 3x or more their creators' medians | Test cheaply within 72 hours, if it fits the brand |
| Rising | 5 to 25 creators, ratios above 2 holding for a week, large accounts mostly absent | Produce your best version this week |
| Peaking | More than 25 creators, brands and large accounts joining, ratios falling | Join only with a clear twist, or skip |
| Fading | New videos fall below creators' medians, imitation outnumbers originals | Retire it from the content plan |

A state is a judgment based on three things you can check: how many distinct creators use it, how its recent videos compare with their creators' norms, and how crowded it is. The comparison against each creator's norm matters because it separates a trend from a creator who always gets views.

## What is the 45-minute weekly routine?

The 45-minute weekly routine is four blocks: pull the last seven days, label lens and state, compare with last week, and decide one test, one watch and one retire. More than three decisions and nothing gets done.

**Minutes 0 to 10: Pull.** Run one research pass over the last seven days, across TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts. Use a prompt that asks for the four lenses explicitly (an example follows).

**Minutes 10 to 25: Label.** Give each candidate a lens and a state. For each, note the evidence: two or three links and the creator count. If you cannot find evidence, the row does not make the page.

**Minutes 25 to 35: Compare.** Open last week's radar. Mark which items changed state. The movement matters more than the list. An item that went from Emerging to Rising is your best action. An item that stayed Rising for four weeks is probably a staple.

**Minutes 35 to 45: Decide.** Choose one item to test, one to watch and one to retire. Assign an owner and a date. More than three decisions and nothing gets done.

## What is the one-page template?

The one-page template is a single table where every row has evidence, a brand-fit score and an action, so the page is a ranking rather than a list of ideas. Copy this into a doc or a sheet.

| Item | Lens | Last week | This week | Evidence (links) | Brand fit (0 to 2) | Action | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| | | | | | | | |

Three columns carry the weight. "Evidence" stops guesswork. "Brand fit" stops you from chasing trends you cannot do well. "Action" forces a decision.

## What does the fit filter decide?

The fit filter decides whether a labeled row earns a production slot. Ask three questions before anything goes into production:

1. **Audience fit.** Would your buyer actually watch this?
2. **Proof fit.** Can your product show something real inside the format?
3. **Speed.** Can you shoot, edit and post in 72 hours?

Two out of three is a maybe. One or none is a skip. A trend that does not fit your product turns into content that gets views and no sales.

## What does the worked example show?

The worked example is an illustrative skincare radar for one late-September week, invented to show how rows move between states, not to report a real niche. The rows below are not measurements.

| Item | Lens | Last week | This week | Action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| "Barrier repair" explainers | Topic | Emerging | Rising | Produce a dermatologist-style explainer with a patch test |
| "Night routine in 3 steps" | Format | Not seen | Emerging | Test one version, three openings |
| Skin-fasting challenge | Topic | Rising | Peaking | Skip, because the claims are hard to support |
| Glass-skin routine | Topic | Peaking | Fading | Retire from the plan |

The brand tests one new format, produces one topic video and drops one stale theme. That is a good week.

## What does the pull prompt ask for?

The pull prompt asks Deep Search ([How Deep Search works](/resources/how-deep-search-works)) for formats, audio, topics and products from the last 7 days, with a creator count, a comparison to each creator's median and links for every item. A prompt for the first step:

> For the sensitive-skin skincare niche, find what changed in the last 7 days on TikTok, Reels and Shorts. List formats, audio, topics and products that appear in at least three unrelated creators' videos. For each, give the creator count, how recent videos compare with their creators' median views, and links. Flag anything new since last week.

ContHunt returns the analyzed set with links, so every row on your radar has evidence behind it. The public Viral Now boards are a good second source. They show top viral videos by niche, which helps you spot a trend from an adjacent niche before it arrives in yours (see [Viral Now](/viral-now)). Save each week's evidence to a board so you can compare it with the next one.

## What are the common failures?

Common failures are the habits that turn the radar into a list of ideas, a late chase, or a week with no memory of the week before.

- **Treating the radar as a list of ideas.** It is a ranking with evidence. If a row has no links, delete it.
- **Chasing peaking trends.** By then your version is the fortieth. Peaking items need a real twist or a pass.
- **Ignoring fading signals.** Retiring is a decision too, and it frees capacity.
- **Skipping the comparison.** A single week's list tells you what is popular. Two weeks tell you what is moving.

## What does the weekly review checklist close?

The weekly review checklist closes the 45-minute routine: every row has one lens, one state, evidence and a decision, and the counts still add up. The week that follows is illustrative. The counts are invented to show the arithmetic. They are not a measured niche, and they are separate from the four-row skincare example above.

Tick the checklist in the same doc as the template before the meeting:

- [ ] The pull covered the last 7 days on TikTok, Instagram Reels and YouTube Shorts, and the prompt asked for formats, sounds, topics and products.
- [ ] Every candidate without two or three links was removed. A row without evidence does not stay on the page.
- [ ] Every remaining row has one lens: format, sound, topic or product.
- [ ] Every remaining row has one state, using the definitions in this guide: emerging (fewer than 5 creators, recent videos at 3x or more of the creator median), rising (5 to 25 creators, ratios above 2 holding for a week), peaking (more than 25 creators, ratios falling) or fading (new videos below the creators' medians).
- [ ] Last week's state is filled in, including "not seen" when the item is new. The comparison is the movement, not a second copy of this week's labels.
- [ ] Brand fit is filled in on the template's 0 to 2 scale. Anything you might produce also passes the three fit questions: audience, proof and a post within 72 hours. Two of three is a maybe. One or none is a skip.
- [ ] The new work is exactly one item to test, one to watch and one to retire, each with an owner and a date. Other rows can stay on the page with no new work.
- [ ] Lens counts sum to the number of rows, and state counts sum to the number of rows. If either sum fails, a row was double-labeled or dropped.

**Illustrative week.** A team pulls 24 candidates and drops 6 because those six have no links. The page holds 18 rows. Check: 24 − 6 = 18.

The 18 rows split by lens into 4 formats, 3 sounds, 7 topics and 4 products. Check: 4 + 3 + 7 + 4 = 18.

The same 18 rows split by state into 5 emerging, 6 rising, 4 peaking and 3 fading. Check: 5 + 6 + 4 + 3 = 18. No row is in two states, because 18 labeled rows are the whole page.

The fit filter runs on the 5 emerging items, the state this guide says to test cheaply within 72 hours if it fits. Three of the five pass at least two of the three fit questions. Two pass one question or none and are skipped. Check: 3 + 2 = 5. The routine still allows only one test, so two of the three that passed do not become extra productions. The one watch item is a rising topic that moved up from emerging last week, not a second emerging test. The one retire item is one of the 3 fading rows. New work is 1 test + 1 watch + 1 retire = 3 decisions. Rows with no new work: 18 − 3 = 15. That matches the rule that more than three decisions and nothing gets done.

Time for this illustrative week matches the four blocks. Pull is 10 minutes, label is 15, compare is 10 and decide is 10. Check: 10 + 15 + 10 + 10 = 45 minutes. Minutes 0 to 10, 10 to 25, 25 to 35 and 35 to 45 are the same blocks: 10, 15, 10 and 10.

The tested item has to satisfy the emerging definition, not merely look small. Illustrative evidence for that one format is four creators, which is fewer than 5:

| Creator | Views on the recent video | Creator median | Outlier ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 24,000 | 8,000 | 3 |
| 2 | 18,000 | 6,000 | 3 |
| 3 | 15,000 | 5,000 | 3 |
| 4 | 9,000 | 3,000 | 3 |

Each outlier ratio is views divided by that creator's median. Creator 1 is 24,000 ÷ 8,000 = 3. Creator 2 is 18,000 ÷ 6,000 = 3. Creator 3 is 15,000 ÷ 5,000 = 3. Creator 4 is 9,000 ÷ 3,000 = 3. All four sit on the 3x line in the emerging definition, so the state is not "a large account posted once." The view total is 24,000 + 18,000 + 15,000 + 9,000 = 66,000. The median total is 8,000 + 6,000 + 5,000 + 3,000 = 22,000. The combined ratio is 66,000 ÷ 22,000 = 3, the same bar as each row. If one cell is edited and the ratio cell is not, this check fails and the row goes back to the log.

The rising watch item has to clear a different bar, and the illustrative count is kept separate so the two states are not mixed. Twelve creators have posted it, which sits inside 5 to 25. Eight of those twelve have a recent video above 2x the creator median, which is the "ratios above 2" sign, and the other four do not. Check: 8 + 4 = 12. Eight of twelve is not a license to call every video in the row a breakout. The state can still be rising while you record that 4 of the 12 are only average for their accounts. Large accounts are still mostly absent, which is the other rising sign in the state table. If next week the creator count goes past 25 and the ratios fall, the same row moves to peaking and the action changes from watch to "twist or skip."

The retire item is 1 of the 3 fading rows, not a guess that a topic feels old. Fading means new videos fall below the creators' medians and imitation outnumbers originals. In this illustrative week the other two fading rows stay on the page with no new work, inside the 15. They are visible so next week's comparison can see whether they stayed fading. An item that stayed rising for four weeks, the case named in the compare block, would be marked as a staple instead of as one of the three decisions.

File the checklist under the table. Next week the first question is whether those 18 rows changed state. Lens totals and state totals should again equal the row count. A page that cannot pass 24 − 6 = 18, or 4 + 3 + 7 + 4 = 18, or 5 + 6 + 4 + 3 = 18, is not ready for the meeting, even if the stories on the rows sound convincing.
